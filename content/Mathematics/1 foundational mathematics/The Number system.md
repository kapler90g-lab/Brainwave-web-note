# The Number System
## revision on the number system
> Numbers are ways of quantifying things.
- We know from previous knowledge about three main types of numbers: The natural(counting) numbers($\mathbb{N}$), the whole numbers($\mathbb{W}$) and the integers($\mathbb{Z}$) 

 **Natural numbers** are sets of numbers that contain all the positive counting numbers, $\mathbb{N}$ = {1,2,3,4,5.....}

**Whole numbers** are numbers that contain all the natural numbers plus the number _zero_, $\mathbb{W}$ = {0,1,2,3,4,5.....}

**Integers** are number systems that contain all the whole numbers plus the counter parts of the positive numbers which are **Negative numbers**, $\mathbb{Z}$ = {.....-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3.....}

there are more additional number systems like the rational and irrational numbers finally providing the **Real Numbers($\mathbb{R}$)**.

## Prime and Composite Numbers 

To understand the **prime and composite numbers** we firstly need to revise on **factors and multiples**
 
- a **factor or a divisor** is a natural number that divides another number without leaving any reminders.
   - for example the factors of 12 are 1,2,3,4,6 and 12. this shows the constitutes of the number 12 that makes it up for example 3 and 4 are the constitutes i.e. factors of 12.
   - a factor always exists less than or equal to the number as it is always the constitute that makes up the number.
- a **Multiple** works the other way around, it is a natural number that results in the multiplications of its factors 
   - For example 12 is the multiple of 3 and 4. and the multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36....etc.
   - is always greater than the number as it is the result of its factors.

Just like how atoms are the building block of matter in chemistry, **prime numbers** are the building block of numbers in mathematics.

> **Prime numbers** are natural numbers greater than 1 that have two district **factors** namely, **1 and itself**.

- every number has at least two factors those are 1 and itself and the numbers that have only this two factors and no more are called **prime numbers**
- 2 is the first prime number. every prime number is odd except 2.

> **Composite numbers** are natural numbers that have more than two factors. they are the composite of prime numbers.

- composite numbers are the numbers that have more than the two common factors and hence are composed of primes.

## The Fundamental Theorem of arithmetic
To understand the real meaning of the fundamental theorem of arithmetic we need to understand the meaning of **Prime factorization**.

- The factors of a composite number are called **prime factors**, if the factors are prime numbers. 
- every composite number can be written as a product of its prime factors for example the prime factor multiplications that result in 20 are $2 \times 2 \times 5$ this way of writing a composite number as a product of its prime factors is known as **Prime factorization**.
- a prime factor for a given number is always unique to that number, in a sense every composite number as a prime factor ID that is unique to it. this is where the **fundamental theorem of arithmetic** comes in and sums everything up.

> **The Fundamental theorem of Arithmetic** states that every composite number can be written as a product of its prime factors which is known as **prime factorization** and every composite number has a unique prime factorization.

- this statement in a more informal way states that prime numbers are the building block of every other number and each number has a unique sequences of prime factors that makes it unique.

## common factors and greatest common factors

> Given two or more natural numbers, a number that is a factor of all of them is known as a **common factor**
- numbers may have more than one common factor, and the greatest of all the common factors is called **==the Greatest common factor(GCF)==**.
- the GCF of two numbers _a_ and _b_ is denoted by **$GCF(a,b)$**.
- two or more natural numbers that have a GCF of 1 are called **relatively prime or coprime**.
- and two numbers are written in their simplest from when the GCF between them is 1 or when they are relatively prime or coprime.
> Thus, **==The Greatest common factor==** of two or more natural numbers is the greatest natural number that is a factor of all the given number.

- we can find the GCF of a number by using the **prime factorization method**, for example the $GCF(10,20,60)$:
  1. list the prime factorization of all of them:
     - 10 = 2x5
     - 20 = 2x2x5($2^2$x5)
     - 60 = 2x2x3x5($2^2$x3x5)
  2. take the common prime factors with the smallest power:
     - here 2 and 5 are common among the three numbers and we take which ever have the lowest power in this case both with a power of 1 
  3. multiply those two common prime factors which results in the GCF:
    - 2x5=10 and thus $GCF(10,20,60)=10$
   

## common multiple and least common multiple

> Given two or more natural numbers, a number that appears as a multiple of all of them is known as a ==**common multiple**==.
- numbers may have more than one common multiple and the smallest of all those common multiples is known as ==**the Least common multiple**==

> For Two natural numbers _a_ and _b_ the LCM is denoted as $LCM(a,b)$, is the smallest multiple of both a and b.



**Example**

Find LCM (8, 9)

**Solution**

Let $M_8$ and $M_9$ be the sets of multiples of 8 and 9 respectively.

$$
M_8 = {8,, 16,, 24,, 32,, 40,, 48,, 56,, 64,, \color{blue}{72},, 80,, 88,, \ldots}
$$

$$
M_9 = {9,, 18,, 27,, 36,, 45,, 54,, 63,, \color{blue}{72},, 81,, 90,, \ldots}
$$

The least common multiple (LCM) of 8 and 9 is the smallest common element in both sets.

$$
\therefore \text{LCM}(8, 9) = \color{bluw}{72}
$$



**Example**

Use the prime factorization method to find $\text{LCM}(9, 21, 24)$.


**Solution**

Prime factorization can also be used to find the LCM of a set of two or more numbers.
A **common multiple** contains all the prime factors of each number in the set.
The **LCM** is the product of each of these prime factors raised to the **greatest number of times** it appears in the prime factorization of the numbers.

We begin by finding the prime factors:

$$
9 = 3 \times 3 = 3^2
$$

$$
21 = 3 \times 7
$$

$$
24 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 = 2^3 \times 3
$$

The prime factors that appear in these factorizations are **2**, **3**, and **7**.

Considering the greatest number of times each prime factor appears, we take:
$2^3$, $3^2$, and $7$ respectively.

Therefore,

$$
\text{LCM}(9, 21, 24) = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 7 = 504
$$


>[!note] for any natural number $a$ and $b$, $GCF(a,b) \times LCM(a,b) = a \times b$

## **Rational Numbers**
> Any number that can be expressed in the form $\frac{a}{b}$ where $a$ and $b$ are integers and $b\neq0$ is called ==**Rational NUmbers**== and is denoted by $\mathbb{Q}$. is the set described by $$\mathbb{Q}
={{\frac{a}{b}: a \quad and \quad b \quad are  \quad \mathbb{Z}  \quad and  \quad b \neq 0}}$$

- all the pervious number systems we described the natural, whole and integer number systems are all described under this definition of **rational numbers** as any number can  be described in the $\frac{a}{b}$ from, Example like the number 7 can be written as $\frac{7}{1}$ and so can the other numbers and thus all the pervious number systems are the subset of the rational number.

- Rational numbers can be described and written in two forms as a **fraction** and as **decimal**
  - for example the decimal 1.3 can be written in a faction from as $\frac{13}{10}$.
- There are three types of factions:
  - **proper factions**: where the denominator is always greater than the numerator, showing that the numerator properly fits in the denominator example like $\frac{3}{10}$ meaning three out of the ten.
  - **Improper faction**: is where the numerator is greater than the denominator example like $\frac{10}{3}$ showing that we need to divide 10 things into three parts.
  - **Mixed factions** are always an improper faction written as a mix of integers and a fraction like $3\frac{2}{3}$ which is equal to the improper faction $\frac{11}{3}$.

- when a rational number is expressed in faction, it is often formally written in its simplest from and as we can recall two numbers are in the simplest from when their GCF is equal to one.
  - for example the simplest form of $\frac{30}{45}$ is $\frac{2}{3}$ which means that if we wanna write $\frac{30}{45}$ in its simplest and lowest from it is $\frac{2}{3}$.

### **Converting factions into decimals**
The faction $\frac{3}{8}$ can be expressed as a decimal by **dividing the numerator by the denominator** which gives us **$0.375$**.
  - a decimal like $0.375$ which has an ending or terminating point during the division is known as a **Terminating decimal**. a decimal is terminating if it hits a reminder of zero at some point.
 
The faction $\frac{7}{12}$ can be expressed as a decimal $0.5833...$ here the digit 3 repeats and the division doesn't terminate.
  - a decimal like  $0.5833...$ is known as a **repeating decimal** as the decimal never terminates and repeats the same number forever.
  - to show a decimal is a repeating decimal we add a bar above the number that is repeating for example  $0.5833...$ can be written as $0.58\overline{3}$, this method of writing a repeating decimal is known as **Bar notation** and the portion of the decimal that repeats is known as **the repetend** for example in the above example 3 is the repetend.

> so, any rational number  $\frac{a}{b}$ can be expressed as a decimal by dividing the numerator a by the denominator b.

- when dividing a by b the following two cases occur:
  - The division process ends or terminates when the reminder of zero id obtained, in this case the decimal is called a **Terminating decimal**
  - The division process doesn't terminate as the reminder never becomes zero such decimals are called **repeating decimals**

### **converting repeating and terminating decimals into factions**
> every terminating decimal can eb expressed a fraction with a denominator of 10,100,1000..so on.
- for example 0.85 can be expressed as  $\frac{85}{100}$. for any d terminating decimal number with a n digits after a decimal point then we can write d as a fraction using:
$$ d = \frac{d \times 10^n}{10^n}$$

> any repeating decimal can be expressed as a faction.
- if d was the repeating decimal with K non-repeating and p repeating digits after the decimal point, then the formula becomes:
$$d=d\frac{(10^{k+p}-10^k)}{10^{k+p}-10^k}$$


so:
1. every rational number can be expressed as a terminating or repeating decimal
2. every terminating or repeating decimal can be represented as a rational number.

## **Irrational numbers** 

> ==**Irrational numbers**== are numbers that cannot be expressed in the form  $\frac{a}{b}$ where a and b are integers and $b \neq 0$.
- irrational numbers are the anthesis of rational numbers as they cannot be expressed as fractions.
- decimals that neither **repeat nor terminate** are known as **Irrational numbers**
- the square root of a non-perfect square number is an irrational number for example $\sqrt{6}$ is an irrational number as 6 is a non-perfect square.

- The sum of an rational number and an irrational number is always irrational.
- the set of rational numbers are enclosed under all of the four operations.
- the set of irrational numbers are not enclosed in any of the four operations.
- the product of a rational and an irrational number is always irrational except when multiplying with zero.

## **Real Numbers**
- from the pervious we have understood that decimal numbers can be either rational or irrational numbers

- it can be shown that every decimal number be it rational or irrational, be associated with a unique point on the number line and conversely every point on the number line can be associated with a unique decimal number either rational or irrational.

> a number is called ==**real number**== if and only if it is either rational or irrational number. 
> The set or real number denoted by $\mathbb{R}$, can be described as a union of the set of rational and irrational numbers.

It is good to understand and appreciate the existence of a one-to-one correspondence between any two of the following sets:

$$
1.\; D = \{ x \mid x \text{ is a decimal number} \}
$$

$$
2.\; P = \{ x \mid x \text{ is a point on the number line} \}
$$

$$
3.\; \mathbb{R} = \{ x \mid x \text{ is a real number} \}
$$

- ordering of numbers on the number line is essential as we have established that every point on the number line contains a unique decimal number which will be either smaller or larger than the next number. here are three of the most important properties of order:

1. **Trichotomy property**
For any real number a and b one only one of the following is true:
$$a<b \quad or \quad a=b \quad or \quad a>b$$

2. **Transitive property of order**
For any three real numbers a,b and c if $a<b$ and $b<c$ then $a<c$.

3. **the squaring order**
For a non-negative real numbers a and b if $a^2>b^2$ then $a>b$
and for a non-positive real numbers c and d if $c^2>d^2$ then $d>c$

## Exponents and radicals 
### Roots and radicals 
if we represented numbers as dots then numbers that can form squares using their dot representation are known as **square numbers** or **perfect squares**
- to form the square using the dots the rows and columns of the square must be a specific number of dots for that given number and are known as **the square root** of the perfect square. for example the perfect square 9 has a square root of 3 meaning we need 3 dots in the row and columns to produce a perfect square using 9 dots.

> for nay real numbers a and b, $a^2=b$, then a is the **square root** of b.

- perfect squares are not only integers they can also be fractions and decimals like 0.09 or $\frac{4}{9}$ 
- the symbol $\sqrt{}$ called **radical sign(meaning root sign)** is used to indicate the square root of a number. for example  $\sqrt{25}$ is named as the square root of 25.
- if b is a positive real number then $\sqrt{b}$ is a positive real number as well, negative real numbers do not have square roots since the square of any real number is always positive, $a^2\geq0$ for any real number a. but this principle doesn't hold when taking any old number root like the **cube root**
e
> for any real number a and b, and a positive integer n, $a^n=b$, then a is the **$n^{th}$ of b**



> If $b$ is any real number and $n$ is a positive integer greater than 1, then, the principal $n^{th}$ root of $b$, denoted by $\sqrt[n]{b}$ is defined as
$$\sqrt[n]{b} = \begin{cases} \text{the positive } n^{th} \text{ root of } b, & \text{if } b > 0. \\ \text{the negative } n^{th} \text{ root of } b, & \text{if } b < 0 \text{ and } n \text{ is odd.} \\ 0, & \text{if } b = 0. \end{cases}$$



i. If $b < 0$ and $n$ is even, there is no real $n^{th}$ root of $b$, because an even power of any real number is a non-negative number.

ii. The symbol $\sqrt{\phantom{x}}$ is called a **radical sign**, the expression $\sqrt[n]{b}$ is called a **radical**, $n$ is called the **index** and $b$ is called the **radicand**. When no index is written, the radical sign indicates square root.

### Meaning of fractional exponents


> If $b \in \mathbb{R}$ and $n$ is a positive integer greater than 1, then
 $$b^{\frac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{b}$$

> For any two real numbers $a$ and $b$ and for all integers $n \ge 2$, $a^{\frac{1}{n}}b^{\frac{1}{n}} = (ab)^{\frac{1}{n}}$.

> For any two real numbers $a$ and $b$ where $b \neq 0$ and for all integers $n \geq 2$,
$$ \frac{a^{\frac{1}{n}}}{b^{\frac{1}{n}}} = \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} $$


>For $a \geq 0$ and $p$ and $q$ any two positive integers, $$ a^{\frac{p}{q}} = \left(a^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^p = (\sqrt[q]{a})^p $$
