#grade10 #unit2 #plants
## 1. **Characteristics** of **Plants**
- Plants are *living things*, they grow, they reproduce, and respond to *changes*.
- Plants are *multicellular*, made up of *many* **eukaryotic** cells.
- Plants are **autotrophic** (*self-feeding*); they have a green pigment known as **Chlorophyll**, which helps to absorb *sunlight* and produce *food*. They are also called **producers**.
- Plants are **sessile**; they cannot move by themselves.
- They reproduce **asexually and sexually**, undergoing *various* reproduction patterns.

## 2. **Flowering** and **Non-flowering** **Plants**
- **Gymnosperms** (*non-flowering* plants) (conifers) 
    - have a *well-developed* root and leaf system but have no **flowers**.
    - Their **reproductive** organ is a **Cone**, instead of **flowers**.
    - The **seeds** produced are in their **cones** and without cover of a **fruit** (naked seeds).
  
- **Angiosperms** (*flowering* plants):
    - They have a *well-developed* root system and leaves that have **flowers**.
    - Produce **seeds** within a **fruit**.
  
## 3. **Structure and Function of Plant Parts**
In this **sub-topic**, we will focus on **external** and **internal** structures of a linear **flowering plant** (**angiosperm**).

The **external** structure of an **angiosperm** contains two types of **systems**:
    - **Root System**
    - **Shoot System**

#### The Shoot System
This is the **plant part** usually found above the ground and includes the organs like **stems**, **leaves**, **buds**, **flowers**.

### The Root System
This is the **plant part** usually found **downwards** into the ground. 
**Roots** are distinguished from an underground stem because they **don’t** bear **leaves** or **buds**.
With regards to **roots**, there are generally two types of roots - namely, **tap-roots** and **fibrous roots**.

#### **Tap-root**
- Consists of one large, many **lateral roots**.
- It has little **lateral root**.
- By penetrating deep into the soil, the **tap root** provides stability (anchorage) and absorbs water located deep in the ground.
- Taproot system is a feature of **dicot plants**.

#### **Fibrous root**
- Is usually formed by thin, moderately ,branching roots growing from stems.
- They are similar in size and length.
- They are very efficient for absorbing **water** and **minerals**.
- They are features of **monocot plants**.

### **Leaf** 

The **external** structures of a leaf consist of a **petiole** (**leaf stalk**), **lamina** (broadest part), **midrib**, **margin**, **base**, and **tips**.

#### **Lamina**
Is the **broadest part**, which is flat, wide, and commonly thin. 
- It is **thinness**, creates short distance for gas exchange through the **stomata** (thin space).

#### **Midrib**
- Is the **harder** and **contain** the **vein** (transparent **vessels**) of the leaf as well as supportive tissues with hard cell wall.

-There are two components of leaves **internal structure**, namely, **outer layer** and **inner layer**
#### Outer Layer
also known as **epidermis**.

- It is a **single** layer of *highly-packed* cells that cover the **upper** and the **lower** *surfaces* of the leaf.
- The **upper epidermis** is usually covered by a **waxy** *cuticle* which transmits *sunlight* for **photosynthesis**, which *resists* water loss by **evaporation** from the leaf, *tissue*.

- The **lower epidermis** usually contains *bean-shaped* **guard** cells that leave open spaces known as **stomata**. 

- **Stomata**, or *little mouths* or *little noses* which regulate *O₂*, *CO₂*, *water* loss.

##### Middle Layer
- Is also known as a **mesophyll** (*middle leaf*) layer.
- It lies in the **upper** and the **lower** *epidermis*.
- It involves tissues that directly or *indirectly* involve in **photosynthesis**.
- There are two regions in this **mesophyll** layer:
    - The **Palisade Layer**
    - The **Spongy Layer**

###### The Palisade Layer
- Is composed of *irregularly* arranged and *closely-packed* columnar (vertically elongated) cells.

- The **cell** contains the **largest** *number* of **chloroplasts** per cell.

###### The spongy layer,
  - has **broad** thin **palisade** cells,
  - they are **irregularly** shaped with **fewer** chloroplasts,
  - they are **loosely** arranged with **numerous** airspaces.
  - Those airspaces which are **very** close to the **stomata**, allow for the diffusion of **O₂** and **H₂O** (g), and **CO₂**.

### **stems**

#### **Epidermis**,
  - is the outer layer of the stem
  - the outer **walls** are generally **thickened** with **cuticles** which minimizes the rate of **transpiration**.
  - the cells are **compactly** arranged which protects the **stem** from injury and harmful organisms.

#### **Hypodermis**,
  - lies **below** the **epidermis**.
  - it is mainly composed of a collencyma cell, which is thickened at the corners due to the deposition of their **cellulose**.
  - enables the **layer** to give **mechanical** strength to the **stem**.

#### **Cortex**
- Consists of a few layers of *thin-walled*, *large*, *roving*, or *oval* *cells*.

#### **Endodermis**
- Is the *inner part* of the **Cortex** that *separates* the **Cortex** from the **vascular bundles**.
- The *cells* are usually **compactly** arranged and usually contain *starch* *grains*.
- Thus, the **endodermis** serve as a *food* *reserve* and may be termed as a ***starch sheath***.

#### **Vascular bundles**
- Along with *strands* of *conducting* *tissues* or *transpiring vessels*, consisting essentially of **xylem** and **phloem**, arranged *around* a central **pith**.
  
    - **Xylem**
    - **Xylem** transports *water* and *dissolved* *minerals* to the **photosynthetic** *tissues*, mainly to the *leaf*.

    - **Phloem**
    - **Phloem** transports *synthesized* *food* to all *tissues*, *storing* for *utilization* or *storage*.

#### **Pith**
- Occupies the *central part* of the *stem*, composed of *thin-walled* *cells*, which are *round* or *polygonal*.

### **Root**

#### **Pelliferous layer**
- They are the *outer most layer*, 
- Consists of a *single-celled root hair*.
- The *cuticle* is - *absent*.

#### **Cortex**
- They are *oval loosely arranged cells with intercellular space* Used to *store food and water*.

#### **Endodermis**
- is the **innermost layer** of the *cortex*.
- made of a **barrel-shaped closely packed cells**.
- The movement of *water and dissolved minerals* from the *cortex into the xylem*.

#### **Pericycle**
- is a **single layer** inner to **endodermis**. It is the *site of origin* of *lateral root*.

#### **Vascular bundles**
- consists of **phloem** and **xylem**.
- with *activity of dividing cells in them*

 

# **Reproductive Structures and Life Cycles of a Gymnosperm**

- The pine tree has male and **female** cones on one **plant**. **Pollen** is transferred from the **male cone** to the **female cone**.The process is called **pollination**.
- The **pollen** will undergo **germination** with the formation of the pollen tube.
- The pollen tube **develops** **slowly** and the **generative** cell of the pollen grain divides into **two sperm cells** as it approaches the **female cone**.
- The **femal** cone consists of **two ovules** per **scale**.
- Each ovule undergoes **meiosis** (**gamete** or **sex cell**) formation by **cell division**, and produces **four cells** per scale.
- Three of the four breaks, leaving us with only a **single surviving cell**.
- the cell **develops** into a **gametophyte** having **archagonium**.
- An **archagonium** is a **reproductive structure** that contains a **single large egg**.
- In this **large egg**, that fuses (**unites**) with one of the sperm cells and forms a **zygote**. This process is known as **fertilization**.
- A **zygote** develops into a **seed** **embryo** inside the **female cone**
- After the seed is **matured**. It **liberates** upon a **drying** and opening of the **female cone**.
- The seed will be **scattered** or **dispersed** away from the **parent plants** and **germinates** into a **seedling** (*young pine plant*).
- Finally, the young plant (*seedling*) grows and develops into a **mature** plant with **male** and **female cones**.

### **Reproductive structure and life cycle of flowering plant**
  - A flower is the **reproductive organ** of flowering plants (*Angiosperm*).
  - Their seed is covered with a **fruit**.
  - A typical flower has **four floral parts**:
    - **Sepal** (*Calyx*)
    - **Petals** (*Corolla*)
    - **Stamen** (*Androecium*)
    - **Pistil** (*Gynoecium or Carpel*)

- **Sepal** (*Calyx*):
    - Usually a *green* leaf-like structure which protects the lower part of the **male** and **female structure** (*part*).

- **Petals** (*Corolla*):
    - Most *bright* color which attracts pollinating agents (*like insects*).

- **Stamen** (*Androecium*):
    - Is the **male** part which consists of the **filament** and **anther**.

- **Pistil** (*Gynoecium or Carpel*):
    - Is the **female part** consisting of:
        - **Stigma**
        - **Ovary**
        - **Style**
        - **Ovule**

# **Pollination**
- This is the transfer of *pollen* from an **anther** of a **stamen** to the **stigma** of the **pistil**.  
There are two types of **pollination**:  
  - **self pollination**  
  - **cross pollination**  

## **Self pollination**
- It is the transfer of *pollen* from the **stamen** to **stigma** of the same **plant**.  

## **Cross pollination**
- It is the transfer of *pollen* from the **stamen** to **stigma** of flowers of different **plants**.  
- **Cross pollination** is most of the time done by *pollinating agents* like *winds*, **bees**, *insects*.  

A *flowering plant* passes through **pollen tubes** described as follows:  
# **Pollen tube formation**
- A *pollen grain* landing on the **stigma** will form *pollen tubes* that grow down in the **style** and form a male **gamete** as it approaches the **ovule**.  

# **Fertilization**
- It is the union of *male gamete* and *female gamete*. *Occurring* in the **ovule** within the **ovary**, as a result, a **zygote** will be formed.
- In **flowering** **plants**, there is a **Second** **fertilization**.  which forms **endosperm** (*a reserve food storage structure*).  Thus, flowering plants **angiosperms** exhibit a **second** (*double*) for **fertilization**.  

The **seed** or **fertilized** **ovule** contains three parts:
    - The **seed** **embryo**
    - The **cotyledon**/**endosperm** (**food** **provisioning**)
    - **Seed** **coat**

In angiosperms, there is an *additional* cover to the **seed** besides to seed coat, the **fruit** Unlike the **gymnosperms**, which doesn't have a **fruit**, only the **seed** **coat**.

- The **seed** **embryo** consists of the **radicle** (*future root*), **hypocotyl**, and **plumule** (future shoot).

**Cotyledons** and **endosperms** are *food* **storing** *tissues*, essential for the **seed** **embryo** (*future plant*) until it forms **leaf** and starts *manufacturing* its own *food*.

A **seed** of an **angiosperm** may have one **cotyledon** (*monocot*) or two **cotyledons** (*dicot*).

# **Seed** and **fruit** **formation**  
Following **fertilization**, **seeds embryo** are formed.  
The **ovule** matures into a **seed**,  
and the **ovary** matures into a **fruit**.  

# **Seed** **dispersal**  
It is a **mechanism** of *scattering* **seeds**.  
**Seeds** disperse like *pollination*, requiring *pollinating agents* like *wind* and *insects*.  

# **Seed** **dormancy** (*germination*)  
The fate of **seed** landing at a certain area is either **dormancy**.  
If a **seed** is **dormant** (*remaining inactive*), then it is *waiting* for a favorable condition to **germinate**.  
If there is a favorable condition, the **seeds** will break **dormancy**,  
and start to form a **seedling** (*a young plant*). This process is known as **germination**.  


## **Seed Dispersal and Germination**

### **Seed dispersal**
is an *adaptive mechanism* of plants that ensures **seeds** will be *separated* from the *parent plants*, distributed over a *large area*.

- By doing this, they *minimize* *competition* at one place. **Seeds** can be *dispersed* by *animals, wind*, or *water*.

### **Seed germination**

- The **resumption** of active growth of the **embryo** after a period of *dormancy* is known as **Germination**.  
- There are three essential conditions for seed **germination**:  
  1. **Water** (*moisture*)  
     - It is important because the *hydration* of the seed *increases* its permeability to **O₂**.  
     - It also acts as a *transporter* for organic substances.  
  2. **Oxygen** (*Aeration*)  
     - It is essential for *aerobic* respiration by which seeds Get energy for the growth of the **embryo**.  
  3. **Temperature** (*Warmth*)  
     - Seeds seek optimum *temp* for **germination**.  
     - The optimum *moisture*, air, and warmth activate the growth of the **embryo**.  

 The outward sign of *growth* is related that *develops* from the **hypocotyl**.  
     - The **hypocotyl** is the first to come out and grows down into the *soil* like a primary root.  
  
# **Photosynthesis**  
 - Plants produce their own food by using **photosynthesis**.  
     - They are also called *producers* or *autotrophs*. 

- The largest source of **Energy** that sustains life begins with **Sunlight**,  
and in turn, this **Sunlight** is trapped by a **Green** pigment, known as **Chlorophyll**.  
- Thus, it occurs into the organisms bearing **Chlorophylls**,  
and then those trapped **Sunlights** are converted into a **Chemical** **Energy**. This whole process is known as **Photosynthesis**.

## The **photosynthetic** **apparatus** (**chloroplasts**)
- We recall that the **mesophyll** layer (the **palisade** layer one),  
contains many **Chloroplasts**. Thus being the best part to trap **Sunlight**.  
- Accordingly, there are two parts in the **photosynthetic** apparatus of **Chloroplasts**. These are **Granum** and **Stroma**.

1. **Granum**
     - It consists of stacks of **flatted Sacks**.  
     - Each of the **flatted** sacks is called **thylakoids**.  
     - It contains the **Chlorophyll** enzymes and other cofactors that participate in the light trapping process.  
     - It is here that the light reaction takes place.  

2. **Stroma**
    - It is a **gel-like** **Colorless** **matrix**.
    - It is a piece for **Sugar (Carbohydrate)** synthesis through **Carbon fixation**.
    - It is the **sugar** produced from that is directly converted in **organic** compounds into amino acids, proteins, and **lipids**, which is **necessary** for *all* organisms.

### **Light-Absorbing System in Chloroplasts**
The **chloroplasts** contain the **chlorophyll** and other **light**-absorbing accessories.

Those accessories absorb most of the **visible light** ranging from **400-700 nm**.

The max **absorption** occurs at the wave length **400-500** nm and **600-700nm**, **blue** and **red** light respectively.

Light ranging from **500-600** nm includes **green** lights.

This is the **reason** why plants look *green*.

## Mechanisms of Photosynthesis
- Mostly it is the sum of the following two sub-reactions:

#### **Light reaction.**
 - Also known as **light dependance** stage.
 - It mostly takes place in the **Granum**.
 - Here this Granum is organized as **photosystem** and **electron transporting system**.
- The **Photosystem** contains **Chlorophyll** which absorbs sunlight maximally at **Blue** and **Red** ranges of the **Spectrum**.  

  **The light absorbs by the Chlorophyll will:**  
    - Split the **water molecules** (H2O) into **H+** and **O2**.  This is also known as **Photolysis**, and the **O2** is released to the **atmosphere** as a byproduct, though, to the **stomata**.  
    - Exits some **electrons**. In the **Chlorophyll** molecule, to **higher** energy levels, and generate a high energy **molecule**, **ATP** molecule. Thus, the **ATP** and **H+** harvested
     during the light reaction will be used as an input for the **stroma** where the conversion of **CO2** into **Sugar** (**Carbohydrate**) takes place.  

#### **Dark reaction**  
  - Also known as **light-independent stage**.  
  - It can occur in the **absence** of light as long as there is a sufficient amount of **H+** and **ATP** from light reaction, the **Dark reaction** and the **enzymatic reaction**, **H+** independently reacts with **CO2** in the **stroma** of **Chloroplast**. This process is known as **Carbon Fixation**.  
  - **Glucose** (**Carbohydrate**) is the immediate result of the dark reaction. 
  - The overall reaction can be summarized as:

$$ 6CO_2 + 6H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 $$


## **Transport System In Plants**

- There are two transporting vessels in plants:
  - **Xylem**
  - **Phloem**

- They are collectively known as **vascular bundles**.

- Water and minerals are transported from the root to the leaf via the stem, through the conducting vessel called **Xylem**,

  - which consists of elongated dead cells arranged end to end to form vessels.

- Organic matter (food) manufactured in the leaf is transported to the rest of the body by a conducting vessel called **Phloem**.

  - Unlike Xylem, Phloem consists of living cells arranged end to end and allows transport of food (sucrose and amino acids).Up and down the plant, this is called **Translocation**.

## **Mechanism of Transport in Plants**:

### Uptake of Water and Minerals

- Water from the soil first enters the root through root hairs This is called **absorption**.

- The water entering the root passes from cell to cell through **Osmosis**; across the cell membranes and cytoplasm. Thus, they move passively (without the need of energy) Across the **root** and reaches the **root xylem**. **Water** in the **root xylem** is pulled **upward**, passively, by **transpiration pull**. This **pull force** is caused by **transpiration**.

- This is the main **force** responsible for the **water** passing out the **way** from the **root** to the **leaves** through the **xylem vessels**.

- During **water** evaporation from the **leaves** sends us a mechanism to **pull** or **drag water** from the **root**.

### **Translocation of organic matter (food)**

**Translocation** in plants is a **shift** or transport of **food** from the **site** of **synthesis (leaf)** to the **site** of **utilization** or **storage (stem and root)**.

**Translocation** occurs through **phloem** Which is made up of **living cells**.

It is an **active transport** where the **living** **phloem cells** use **energy** obtained by **metabolic process**.

## **Tropism as growth response**

Plants like **other living things** respond to **stimuli** that is the **change** in the **environment**.

**Tropism** is **bending** towards (**positive response**) or **away** (**negative response**) to the direction of the **stimulus**.  
The cause of the response is **unilateral** **Stimulus** (coming from one side only).  
Which causes **unequal distribution** of **growth hormone**, resulting in **unequal growth**. This means one side grows more or less than the other side.  
**Tropism** is exhibited by the **shoot** and **root** of a **plant** due to unequal concentration of **growth hormone**, commonly **auxin**.

**Auxin** is particularly **Indole Acetic Acid** (**IAA**) is **plant growth hormone**
It is **transported** to the region of **active growth** and affects **cell elongation**.

- **there are three main types of tropism:**  
    - phototropism
    - geotropism
    - hydrotropism

### **Phototropism**  
is when the **plant responds** to **light** mainly exhibited  by the **shoot** is positively **phototropic**. early experiment was **conducted** by **Charles Darwin**. **Positively *phototropic** means that the **shoots** will **bend** towards, the source of **light** (i.e., the **hormone** **auxin** which **activity** grows the **place** of more **light** or elongates the cells which are exposed to the **source** of **light**)

### **Hydrotropism** 
- It is when the **root system** bends towards the **source** of **water**.
- It depends upon the **ability** of the **root** to **obtain** **water** and **minerals** from the **soil**.
- **Roots** search for and **grow** towards **water** because it is needed for **photosynthesis**.

### **Geotropism**
- We know that **seed that develops into a **shooting** will form a **root** from the **radicle** and **shoot** from the **plumule**.
- How **shooting** stands erect is the **result** of a **response** to **gravity**, known as **geotropism**.
- The **root** from the **radicle** bends **downwards** while the **shoot** bends **upwards** until the **root-shoot** **system** orients **itself**.
- In **upright** position, when there is a **uniform** effect of **gravity**, 
- **clinostat** is an **material** that **forces** a **plant** horizontally by spinning. It applies equal **gravitational** **force** to all **sides**.
