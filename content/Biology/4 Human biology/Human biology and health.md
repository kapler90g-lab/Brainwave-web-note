#  **Food** and **Nutrition**

- **Food** is anything taken in to provide *energy* for the **Body**.

**Food** contains *six* major groups of **nutrients**:
- **Carbohydrates**
- **Protein**
- **Fat** (*lipid*)
- **Vitamins**
- **Minerals**
- **Water**

- **Nutrients** that are needed by the **Body** can be grouped as:

- **Micronutrients** – are groups of **nutrients** that are needed by the **Body** in a *trace* or *small amount*.

- **Macronutrients** – are groups of **nutrients** that are needed by the **Body** in a *large amount*.

## **Carbohydrates**

- are a *quickest*, most *readily* available source of *energy* for the **Body**.
- are composed of **three** elements: **Carbon**, **Hydrogen**, **Oxygen**.

- The general formula of **Carbohydrates** is:
$C_nH_2nO_n \quad for \quad (n > 3)$

**Carbohydrates** are also the **storage** forms of food in *plants* and *animals*.

- **Starch** is the **storage** form of **carbohydrates** in *plants*.
- **Glycogen** is the **storage** form of **carbohydrates** in *animals* (**animal starch**).

**Carbohydrates** are classified into *three major groups* based on the *number of sugar units* which they contain:

1. **Monosaccharides** (*simple sugar*)
2. **Disaccharides** (*double sugar*)
3. **Polysaccharides** (*complex sugar*)

### **Monosaccharides** (*simple sugar*)

- Are *simple* and **soluble** forms of **carbohydrates**.
- They are *composed* of *one sugar unit*.

>[!note] **Sugar** units are also called **saccharides**.

- The most known **examples** are **glucose, fructose**, and *they* have **similar molecular formula** **$(C_6H_{12}O_6)$** or *that* are structurally different such **kinds of molecules** are called **somers**.

- **Isomers** are molecules that have *similar molecular* formulas, but *different* structural molecules.

All **monosaccharides** are **soluble** in *water* and *have* **sweet** taste.
### **Disaccharides** (Double Sugar)

- These are groups of **carbohydrates** which are composed of **two** sugar units.
- They are formed by a **condensation reaction** between two **monosaccharides**.
  
  **Condensation reaction** is a reaction that produces **water** as a **byproduct**. (Waste)
  
  **C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O → C₁₂H₂₁O₁₁ + H₂O**
 ^5faffc
- The most known examples of disaccharides are:
  
  - **Sucrose** formed by **glucose** and **fructose** as a **table sugar**.
  
  - **Lactose** formed by **glucose** and **galactose** as a sugar found in **milk**.
  
  - **Maltose** formed by **glucose** and **glucose** as a sugar found in **malt**.
  
- All disaccharides are **soluble** in **water** and have a **sweet taste**.
###  **Polysaccharides** (Complex Sugars)

These are groups of **carbohydrates** which consist of long chains of **sugar units** (monosaccharides).

**Polysaccharides** are stable forms of **carbohydrates** which can be used directly by the body

They are storage forms of **carbohydrates** in our bodies.

They are also the major components of **cell walls** of plants and some *bacteria*.

**Polysaccharides** can be *insoluble* in *water* and have no sweet to taste.

 Some Examples Include:

- **Starch**: Found mainly in *food items* such as **wheat**, **potatoes**.
    - One storage form of **carbohydrates** in plants.
    - Plants store **starch** in parts like *tubers*, *roots*, and *fruits*.
  
- **Glycogen**: Storage form of **carbohydrates** in *animal tissues*.
    - They are stored mainly in *liver* and *muscles*.
  
- **Cellulose**: The major component of **cell walls** in plants.

- **marein**: are component of **bacterial** *cell walls*.

- **Polysaccharides** are **polymers** which are *composed* of monomer units of monosaccharide by condensation reaction.  


## **Protein**

- **Proteins** are organic **substances** that are composed of **elements**: **Carbon**, **hydrogen**, **oxygen**, and **nitrogen**. Sometimes, it includes **phosphorus** and **sulfur**.

- **Amino acids** are the **building blocks** of **protein**. There are **20** types of **amino acids** that are joined to one another and form **protein**.

- The bond between **amino acids** is called a **peptide link**. The bond in **amino acids** is an example of **condensation reaction**.

- There are various types of **proteins** which differ from one another by **number** and **sequence** of **amino** **acid** residues.
- Some **proteins** - one **insoluble** in water, while others are **soluble** in water.
- **water insoluble proteins** are **structural** proteins that are used to **make** **connective** tissues, **tendons**, **bone matrix**, **hair**, **skin**, and **function**.
- **Water-soluble proteins** form **antibodies**, **enzymes**, and **some hormones**.
- It is used to **build** and **repair** the body **tissues**.
- Some **stuffs** that have a lot of **protein** content are **egg**, **fish**, **chicken** meat.

## **Fats and Oils**
- **Fats** are groups of **nutrients** that are composed of **carbon**, **hydrogen**, and **oxygen** In fats is *little Oxygen* compared to **carbohydrates**.
- They are **important** for:
  - *Source of energy*.
  - **Structural** components of **cell membranes**.
  - **Insulation** of *layers* under **skin**.
  - **Regulation** of **body** temperatures.
- **Fats** contain *twice as much energy than that of carbohydrates*.
- Solvents **which** are **only** **organic**: Solvents like *alcohol*.
  
- **Lipids** are *usually* found in *food* *sources* like *butter*, *fatty* *meats*, *vegetable* *oils*, and **group** nuts.
  
- **Lipids** are built from two kinds of **subunits**:  
  - **fatty** **acid**  
  - **glycerol**

### **Glycerol**
- a **three** - Carbon *alcohol*. With each *Carbon* *bearing* a *Hydroxyl* **(–OH)** *group*.
  
- Also three *Carbons* form the **Back** *Bone* of the *fatty* *molecule* to *which three **Fatty** **acids** are *attached*.  
  Their *molecular* formula is **$C₃H₈O₃$**.

### **Fatty acids**
- Long **chain** of **hydrocarbons** (consisting of one **Carbon** and *hydrogen* *atoms*), ending with a **carboxyl** *(-COOH)* *group*.

- **Fatty** **acids** vary in *length*, the most group of *fatty* **acids** are *even*, numbered *ranging* of **14** to **20** Carbons.

- The **fatty** **acids** are *classified* as **saturated** and **unsaturated** *fatty* **acids**.

#### **Saturated** **fatty** **acids**
- *contain* *single* *bonds* *between* **Carbon atoms** Containing internal Carbon atoms having two **hydrogen**, **Side Groups**,
- are mostly **solid** at room **temperature**. 

The more **saturated** the **fatty acid**, is the more **richer** to energy. But they may upset the normal **balance** of fatty acids in the **body** is a situation that can lead to heart **disease**.

## 6. **Water**

- It is mainly **important** as a **constituent** of **living** cells and **habitat** for most *organisms*.
- **Biological** uses of **water**:
  - It’s a **universal** *solvent*.
  - It ***carries*** *affects* *in* our **body**, through *evaporation*.
  - Involved in **transport** of **substances** in the **body**.
  - ***Serves*** as an **immune** *factor*, taking waste products in the form of ***solution*** in our **blood**
  - **Hydrolysis** reaction: it **hydrolyzes** large food **molecules** during *digestion*.
  - **Removes** *wastes* (both **nutritious**) from the **body** in the form of **juices** or *sweat*.
- For **osmoregulation** - maintaining *regular* **balance** of the **body**.

### **Requirements**
- It is the **bulk** of **endogenous** food *minerals*.
- the human digestive system cannot digest cellulose thus thus, most of the vegetables remain undigested and are called fibers or roughage

- **Constipation** - *during* **removal** *of* **feces**
- feces that stay too long in the large intestine causes most of its water to be absorbed and becomes compact, hard and deficits to evacuate from the body 

### Nutrition
- **Nutrition** 
    - Is the process of obtaining and utilizing food, and it involves:
        - **Ingestion** → **Digestion** → **Absorption** → **Assimilation**
        
        - **Ingestion**: Taking the food to the *mouth*.
        
        - **Digestion**: Changing *large* complex *and* indigestible food 
          into *small* complex *and* soluble food *particles*.
        
        - **Absorption**: The process in which *digested* food 
          is *absorbed* in the *blood* stream.
        
        - **Assimilation**: Utilization of food by the *cell*.

### Malnutrition
- **Malnutrition**: Is any *imbalance*  *related* to the 
  *eating* styles/diseases.
    - **Undernutrition**
    - **Overnutrition**
    - **Eating wrong food**
    
    - **undernutrition**: Is the food intake is *too little*, 
      than the food needed in the *body*.
    
    - **Overnutrition**: Is the food intake is *too much*.

## **The Organ Systems**  
The **organ System** is formed when **all** types of organs work together. Examples:  
- **Digestive System**  
- **Circulatory System**  
- **Immune System**  
- **Respiratory System**  
- **Musculoskeletal System**  

### **Digestive System**  
is the *process in which large, complex, and insoluble* particles are changed to their **smaller, simpler** form that can be used up for our *body*.  
The process can be:  
- **Mechanical (Physical) Digestion** - involves *breaking down* of large food into **smaller pieces**.  
- **Chemical Digestion** - involves *converting food* through **hydrolysis reaction**.  
>[!note] Hydrolysis reaction, it’s a *reaction* which involves *splitting* of large molecules into **smaller pieces** (monomers) upon the *addition* of water.


# Enzymes

- **Enzymes** are *complex proteins* that are *produced by the body* to bring about *changes* and **speed up** (catalyze) the *rate of chemical reactions*.
- They are also called **biological catalysts**.

## Types of Enzymes

1. **Extracellular enzymes** - are *enzymes that work outside the cell*.

   *Example: Digestive system.*

2. **Intracellular enzymes** - are *enzymes that work inside the cell*.  
   *Example: Respiratory system.*

## Properties of Enzymes

- **Enzymes** are *complex proteins*.
- **Enzymes** are **biological catalysts**.
- **Enzymes** are *specific (important)*.
- **Enzymes** can be *re-used*.
- **Enzymes** are *sensitive* to *temperature* and **pH**.

# Gut

- **Gut** is a *hollow tubular structure which extends from the mouth to the anus*. It includes:

  - *Mouth* → **Esophagus** → **Stomach** → *Small Intestine →***Large Intestine** → *Anus*.

The whole *digestive system* starts and ends at the **GUT**.
