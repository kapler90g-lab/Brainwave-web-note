# The Digestive System
- During **digestion**, food particles are broken down into *smaller components*, which are *later absorbed* by the body.
- This happens by a **physical means**:
      - **Chewing** and *crushing*
- and by a **chemical means**:
    - via **enzyme-catalyzed reactions**.
- This happens in the **human digestive system**.
- The **human digestive system** is composed of *four digestive processes*.
     - **Ingestion** 
    - **Digestion**
    - **Absorption**
    - **Egestion**

1. **Ingestion** - The taking of *nutrients* in the *mouth*.

2. **Digestion** - The breakdown of *Complex Organic Molecules* into *smaller* components by *enzymes* through the **physical** and **chemical** *process*, from *oral cavity* to the **small intestine**

3. **Absorption** - The transport of *digested nutrients* from the **small intestine** to the *cells* of the *body* through a **finger-like projection** called **villi**. In the **small intestine**, **villi** is a small **finger-like projection** that increases the *surface area* that are covered with **microvilli** with a specialized cell called **epithelium** cells.

4. **Excretion** - The removal of *food* *waste* from the *body*.
- The distinction between **Excretion** and **Egestion** is based on the type of *wastes* removed from the *organism*.

    - Undigested *food* that remains after digestion is *expelled* In *animals*, during **Egestion** process, through the *anus*.

    - **Excretion** is the process through which *metabolic* wastes are **expelled**. In both *animals* and *plants*, through **kidneys**, *skin*, **liver**, and **lung**.


**Chemical** and **Physical** **Digestion**.
>[!note] The *digestive system* of *humans* is *6.5 m to 9 m long*.

**physical** (mechanical) **digestion** begins in the mouth, where food is *chewed* and formed a **bolus**, by *tongue*. A **bolus** is formed as a *ball-like* mixture of food and *saliva*. In the oral cavity, which is usually **swallowed** to the **esophagus**. **Physical** process *breaks down* food into *smaller pieces*, increasing the **surface area** of **chemical digestion**.

**Chemical** **digestion** of **carbohydrate** (starch) starts in the oral cavity by an **enzyme** called **Salivary Amylase** or *ptialin*. 

Thus food is then *swallowed* and enters the **esophagus** - is a long tube that connects the mouth to the **stomach**.

Using **peristalsis**, a wave-like *smooth muscle* contraction, that moves food and other *material* through the **digestive tract**, the muscles of the **esophagus** push the food towards the **stomach**.

The **stomach** contains an extremely *acidic* (pH between is 1.5 and 2.5, a typical *acidity* of gastric juice). 

The **chemical digestion** of **proteins** starts in the **stomach** by *enzyme* **pepsin**.

**Enzyme** *pepsin* is a **digestive enzyme** primarily found in the **stomach**, playing a **critical role** in *breaking down* **protein** and it is more effective at the **pH** of *1.5 to 2*.
    - **Pepsin** is *inactive* when it enters *higher pH* values, like in **small intestine**. 

Further breakdown of food takes place in the **small intestine**, where *bile* (a **greenish-yellow** fluid produced in the **liver** and stored in **gallbladder**) and **enzymes** produced by **small intestine** and **pancreas** continue the process of *digestion*.

**Smaller molecules** are absorbed into the **blood stream** through the **epithelial cells** lining the wall of **small intestine** which are specialized *with* **villi**.

The undigested materials travel through the **large intestine** (*colon*) where *water* is absorbed and *waste materials* are compacted into **feces**. It is stored in the **rectum** until it is *excreted* through the **anus**.

# The **Esophagus**

- The allowed food moves from the *mouth* to the *stomach* by the way of **Esophagus**.
- The bolus of the food **stretches** the walls of the **esophagus**, activating *muscles* that set up *waves of rhythmic contractions* called **peristalsis**.
- **Peristaltic contractions** which make are *involuntary*, move food along the *gastrointestinal tract*. **Peristaltic movement** or *action* will move food from the **esophagus** to the *stomach*.

# The **Stomach**

- is the **site** of food *storage* and initial *protein digestion*.
- It contains *three layers* of **muscles**, which run in *different* directions.
- The movement of *food* to and from the *stomach* is regulated by a *circular* **muscle** called **sphincters**.
- **Sphincters** act like the *drawstring on a bag*.
  1. Contraction of the *lower* **esophageal sphincter (LES)** closes the *entry* of the **stomach**, while its *relaxation* allows food to **enter**.
  2. The **pyloric sphincter** regulates the movement of *food* and **stomach** acids (like **pepsin**) into the *small intestine*.
![[Pasted image 20240913135313.png|300]] 
- **Duodenum** is the *first part* of the **Small Intestine** where it *absorbs* **nutrients** and *relieves* partially digested food.
- **LES**, the *link* between **Esophagus** and **Stomach**.
- **Pyloric Sphincter**, *controls* **Stomach** *emptying into* **Duodenum**.

- The **J-shaped Stomach** has *numerous* **ridges** that allow it to *expand* so that it can store *1.5L* of food.
- Millions of **cells** line the *inner walls* of the **Stomach**

- *activities* in the **Stomach**.
    - The **cells** secrete the *various* **stomach fluids** called **Gastric Juices** that aid *digestion*.
    - contraction of the **Stomach** mix the *food* with the **Gastric fluids (Juice)**.
    - It involves in both *physical* (churning action of **Stomach** wall) and *chemical* digestion (the digestion of proteins by **enzyme pepsin**).

- Approximately *50ml* of **fluids** in the **Stomach** are *produced*.

Gastric fluids include **mucus**, **pepsinogens**, **HCl**, and other minor substances.

- **HCl** kills many substances that are ingested with food.
- It converts **pepsinogen** into its active form called **pepsin**.
- **Pepsin** is a protein digestive enzyme.
- They break the long chains in proteins into shorter chains called **polypeptides**.
- The pH inside the stomach normally ranges between 2 and 3 but may also approach 1.
- Its high acidity that makes it effective at killing **pathogens** and allows **pepsin** to do its work.

- A **thick** layer of **alkaline mucus** protects the stomach lining from being digested.
- The **pepsinogen** passes across the cell membrane and is activated by **HCl** to become its active form **pepsin**.
- The **pepsin** breaks down the proteins in the food but not the proteins of the stomach's cells. Because those proteins are protected by the mucus layer.
- The **esophagus** does not have a protective mucus layer, so if the lower **esophageal sphincter (LES)** is weak the **esophagus** and its lining might be damaged by the **stomach's** acid. **entering** through it, and this causes a pain known as **Heartburn**.

The partially digested food and **gastric** juice mixture are called: **Chyme** which most of the time passes through our **duodenum**.

**Gastric** emptying occurs within two or six hours after a meal. Only a small amount of **Chyme** is released into the **small intestine** at a time. The movement of **Chyme** into the small intestine is regulated by *hormones*, **stomach** *distension*, and *muscle* reflexes that influence the **pyloric sphincter**.

The low pH of the **stomach** (1.5–2.5) will denature the salivary amylase (Pitalin) and lipase that were secreted in the mouth Therefore, over time, the **chemical** digestion of **starches** and **fats** will decrease in the **stomach** and further digestion will take place in the **small intestine**.

#### **The Human Circulatory System**
 The Human Circulatory System is a **double circulatory** system.

It has two **separate** circulations and blood **presses** twice through:

- **Pulmonary Circulation**
- **Systemic Circulation**

##### Pulmonary Circulation

The movement of blood from the heart to the **lungs** and back again into the heart is called **Pulmonary Circulation**.

*Oxygen-depleted* blood (*deoxygenated blood*) from the body leaves the **Systemic Circulation** when it enters the right atrium through the **Superior** and **Inferior** **Venae Cavae**. 

The blood is then pumped into the **tricuspid valve** into the right ventricle, from the right ventricle blood is pumped through the **pulmonary valve** into the pulmonary **arteries**.

##### Systemic Circulation

is the movement of blood from the heart through the body while bringing *oxygenated* blood back to the heart.

*Oxygenated* blood enters the left atrium from the pulmonary vein.
The blood is then pumped into the *left ventricle* through the **bicuspid** (*mitral*) valve; from the left ventricle, blood is pumped through the **aortic valve** and into the **aorta**.

- **Aorta** is the largest artery in *human* body.
- The **aorta** arches and branches into **major** arteries to the *upper* body. Before the *passing* through the *diaphragm*, where it branches into **iliac**, **renal**, **suprarenal** arteries, which supply *lower* parts of the body.
- The arteries branch into *smaller* arteries called **arterioles**, and finally, **capillaries**.
- Gas and nutrient exchange occurs within the **capillaries**. Metabolic wastes and CO₂ diffuse out of the *[[Cell Biology]]* into the blood, while *oxygen* and **glucose** in the blood diffuse out of the blood into the *cells*.
- Systemic circulation loops, the metabolism of every tissue and **organ** alive. **Except** the *parenchyma* of the lung, which is supplied with the *pulmonary* circulation.
#### **Cardiac Cycle**
- The period of time that begins with *contraction* of **atria** and ends with **ventricular** relaxation is known as **cardiac cycle**.
- The period of *contraction* of the heart undergoes while it pumps blood into the circulation is called **systole**.
- The period of *relaxation* that occurs as the chamber is filled with blood is called **diastole**.

- In *normal* **healthy** heart, there are *only two audible sounds*:
  - **Lub and Dup (Dub)**.
    - **Lub** (1st **heart sound**) is the sound created by the closing of **atrioventricular** valves during *ventricular contraction (systole)*.
    - **Dup** (2nd **heart sound**) is the sound created by the closing of the **semi-lunar** valves during *the ventricular relaxation (diastole)*.

- **Murmur**
    - **Murmur** is used to *describe the unusual sound coming from the heart that is caused by the turbulent flow of blood*.
  - **Murmur** is *graded on the scale of* **1 to 6**:
    - 1 being *the most common and most difficult* sound to detect
    - 6 is the most *severe* and easy to detect.

- **Specialized electronic stethoscopes** are used to *record both normal and abnormal sounds created by our heart*.
  - When using a **stethoscope** to *listen to the heart, it is* called **Auscultation**

- **Inhalation** increases the blood flow into the right side and may *increase* the amplitude of right sided heart *murmur*.
  
- **Expiration** partially reduces blood flow into the left side of the heart and may *amplify* the left sided heart *murmurs*.

#### **Blood pressure**

- **Blood pressure** is the [[Force and Newton's law of motion]] of the blood on the wall of the *arteries*.

- And it can be measured indirectly with an *instrument* called **Sphygmomanometer**.

- A gauge on the **sphygmomanometer** measures the pressure **exerted** by the blood during ventricular *contraction* This pressure is called **Systolic blood pressure**.

    - Normal **systolic blood pressure** is less than *120 mm Hg*.

- **Blood pressure** is measured with a non-SI unit called **mmHg**, at some points, blood flows into the artery during *ventricular* relaxation or filling. This pressure is called **diastolic blood pressure**.

    - Normal **diastolic blood pressure** is less than *80 mm Hg*.

- A **Systolic BP** of **120 mmHg** and a **Diastolic BP** of **80 mmHg** would be reported as **120/80**.

- *Reducing* fillings, such as that *caused* by internal hemorrhage will cause a diastolic blood pressure  
### **Blood Vessels**

- They are **channels** or **conduits** through which **blood** is distributed to *body parts* and *tissues*.
- The **vessels** make up the **closed system** of tubes that starts and ends at the *heart*.

  - One **system** called: The **pulmonary vessels** transports **blood** from the *right ventricle* to the *lung* and back to the *left atrium*.
  
  - Other **system** called: **Systematic vessel** carries **blood** from the *left ventricle* to the *other tissues* in all *parts of the body* and then returns **blood** into the right *atrium*.

- Based on their **structures** and **functions**, blood **vessels** are classified as:
    - **arteries**
    - **capillaries**
    - **veins**

#### **Arteries**

- Carry **blood** away from the *heart*; so they have to be able to withstand the **pumping** of the *heart* forcing **blood** out into the *circulation*
- This is usually **oxygenated blood** which is bright and red.
- Their walls are *thick* that contain *muscle* and *elastic* Fibers
- So that they can *stretch* as the blood is forced *through* them and go *back* into their *shape* afterwards.  
- **Arteries** have *pulses*.  
>[!Pulses]
> are the *surge* of the blood *from* the **heart** when it *beats*.   ^8cb495
- The only **arteries** that carry deoxygenated blood are:  
    - **Pulmonary arteries**  
    - **Umbilical arteries**  

- **Pulmonary arteries** carry *deoxygenated* blood *away* from the **heart** to the **lung**.  
- **Umbilical arteries** carry *deoxygenated* blood *away* from the **placenta**.  

**Summary of Arteries**  
- Carry *oxygenated* blood *except*: **pulmonary** and **umbilical arteries**.  
- They are **thick**, **elastic**, and **smooth muscle**.  
- *High pressure* on the **walls**.  

#### **Veins**  
- They carry *deoxygenated* blood *towards* the **heart**.  
- It is usually *low* in **oxygen** and *deep purple color*.  
- They are *thinner* than **arteries**, and the walls are under much low pressure 
They don't have *pulses* But they have **valves** to prevent *back flow.*  
- The only **veins** that carry **oxygenated blood** are;  
    - **The pulmonary veins.**  
    - **The umbilical veins,**  
**Pulmonary veins** carry *oxygenated blood* towards the **heart** from the *lung.*
