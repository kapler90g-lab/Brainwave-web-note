## Mendelian inheritance

an Austrian monk **Gregor Mendel** firstly explained the way in which how traits or characteristics are passed down from parents to organisms or generations to generations and discovered the main principles of inheritance.

he performed numerous experiments with crossing garden peas(Pisum sativum) which spanned a total of 8 years, he later discovered the principles of inheritance and coined different terminologies used in modern genetics.

the **principles of mendelian inheritance** are:
- the law of segregation
- the law of independent assortment

mendelian inheritance refers to the pattern of inheritance of traits or trait transmission controlled by genes with two alternative version called **alleles** from parent to offspring.

---

### Mendelian crosses

Mendel, considered the father of genetics, conducted experiments on pea plants by cultivating and crossing them with each other and observing the different patterns of inheritance at different generations

Mendel took seven traits contained by seven different genes each of which contains different version of the same trait known as **alleles**, those seven genes mainly contain two alleles of contrasting traits.

- **Example**: stem length is a gene with two alleles either a **tall** or a     **short** allele and those traits are contrasting traits

and both of the traits cannot be expressed at the same time as they are contrasting so Mendel hypothesized that there were two alleles(factors) that controlled the height of the stem where one of the alleles has not disappeared but masked by the other.

- he called the allele that is masked **recessive** and the one that masks the recessive or is always expressed the **dominant**.

to test this hypothesis he conducted different types of crosses with the seven characteristics those crosses are monohybrid crosses, dihybrid crosses and test crosses

#### Monohybrid cross

> Monohybrid (monogenic) inheritance involves the transmission of one gene from parents to their offspring.

is the cross between two parents to study the transfer of one trait from each parent to the offspring.

Mendel performed seven crosses for each of the seven characteristics each containing two alleles of contrasting behavior.

Mendel first performed a self-cross that is a tall with a tall, a white with white.. etc this helped him ensure that the pea plants are of a **pure/true line or a purebred** meaning that the traits are similar and no different allele exist on that single gene, as a result the cross or bred between the tall and the tall produces a tall showing no change.

then he began his experiment by cross-pollinating the pure/true lines for contrasting traits such as tall pea plants and short the resulted offspring are called **F1(first filial/progeny ) Generation** in this generation only one of the alleles where shown while the other one disappeared, this is because one of the alleles are always expressible or **dominant** while the one that appears to vanish is called a **recessive**.

he took the F1 generations and he self-pollinated those plants to give rise to an **F2(second filial) generation** in this generation both of the alleles of the genes appeared and he counted the number of F2 progenies with the dominant and recessive trait and found it to be 3:1 ratio of dominant to recessive traits.

- he called the characters that appear at F1 **Dominant** and denoted them with capital letters for tall(T)

- he called the one that appear at F2 generation but not F1 **recessive** and denoted with small letters for short or dwarf(t)

A chart used to designate the total possibilities of crosses for a given trait and used to determine the ratios of the possible genotypes and phenotypes in the offspring of two parents is called a **Punnett square**

>**Phenotype**

> **Genotype**
> is an organisms genetic makeup-- its alleles e.g. TT, tt or Tt
> the genotype of an organism can either be homozygous where the alleles are the same e.g. TT or tt or heterozygous with different alleles e.g. Tt

first two different purebreds are crossed those purebreds are always homozygous either a homozygous dominant e.g. TT or a homozygous recessive e.g. tt they form a F1 generation bred which are always heterozygous dominant e.g. Tt when using a Punnett square the first column and row shows the gametes (which only contain one allele) of the parental generation, the box formed contains the first generation progeny

### the law of segregation

> [!note]- the law of segregation
> - also known as **Mendel's first law of heredity**
> states that a diploid organism of either parent only transfers one random allele from a given gene to the offspring
>the law states that copies of genes randomly separate or segregate during meiosis so that each gamete receives only one allele.
![[Pasted image 20250713093906.png]]
> The Law of Segregation states that each characteristic is determined by a gene, which exists in alternative forms called alleles. During meiosis, the two alleles for each gene segregate so that each gamete receives only one allele.

### the law of independent assortment

> [!note]- the law of independent assortment
> also known as the **Mendel's second law of heredity**
> the **law of independent assortment** state that the inheritance of alleles for one characteristic doesn't affect the inheritance of alleles for another characteristic weather a plant is tall or short for example has no effect upon weather its seeds are round or wrinkled, all of those genes assort or separate independently

> different genes of different traits are independent of each other and are randomly assorted
> genes do not affect each other when assorted into alleles of a gamete

During metaphase I of meiosis I, pairs of homologous chromosomes line up along the cell's equator. However, whether the paternal or maternal chromosomes appears on the left or right is completely random. As a result, which chromosomes end up in each daughter cell is also random. This principle is known as independent segregation or random assortment.

Human cells contain 23 pairs of homologous chromosomes and so independent segregation produces a huge number of different chromosome combinations. Gametes with different chromosome combinations increase genetic variation of offspring.

### Test cross

> [!note] 
> A test cross involves breeding an organism that has a dominant phenotype but an unknown genotype with an organism that is homozygous recessive. This allows us to determine the first parent's unknown genotype.
> it is used to determine the unknown genotype of an organism by crossing it with a known homozygous recessive genotype.

there are two possible Outcomes:

- when crossing the known homozygous recessive with the unknown parent resulted in the formation of all heterozygous dominant plants then the unknown parents genotype is **homozygous dominant**

- when crossing the known homozygous recessive with the unknown parent resulted in the formation of two heterozygous dominant and two homozygous recessive then the unknown parents' genotype is **heterozygous**

**Example:**
if a plant has a tt genotype and is crossed with an unknown genotype plant and produced Tt, Tt, Tt, Tt genotype then the unknown plants genotype must be **homozygous dominant** but if instead produces Tt, Tt, tt. tt then the unknown plant's genotype is **heterozygous**


# sex determination

**sex** refers to the biological attribute the is mostly categorized as male or female.

sex determination is remarkably diverse:

- **hermaphroditic species** are species that contain sex of both male and female
- whole organisms that have separate male and female sexes are called **dioicous**
- **hyplodiploidy** in which male develop from unfertilized egg and are haploid and females develop from fertilized egg and are diploid

the sex of humans and most mammalians is determined by **sex chromosome**

**sex chromosomes** are two chromosomes that can determine weather an offspring is a male or a female those are X and Y chromosomes, in humans out of the 23 pair of chromosomes the 22 are autosomal chromosomes and the 23rd chromosome is a sex chromosome

- individuals with two X chromosomes(XX) are female
- and individual with one X and one Y chromosomes (XY) are male

during meiosis the sex chromosome pairs of an organism separates into gametes, the male separates into X and Y gametes where half of the sperm contains an X chromosome and the other half contains Y chromosome.
and females contain both X chromosomes so they pass X chromosome to the gamete or egg cells

- when the egg is fertilized by an X-bearing sperm becomes a female (XX)
- when fertilized by an Y-bearing sperm it becomes a male (XY)
