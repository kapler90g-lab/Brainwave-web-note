## **The Genetic Materials**

- The branch of biology concerned with the study of the **genetic materials** of organisms and how **traits** are passed from one generation to the next generation through **genes** is called *Genetics*

     -  **Genetic Materials of Organisms** – The main genetic material in organisms is **DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)**, which carries the instructions needed for growth, development, and function, and  **RNA (Ribonucleic Acid)** is the genetic material in some viruses like HIV and COVID-19 instead of DNA.
     
     - **Traits** – Traits are characteristics that an organism inherits, such as eye color, hair type, height, or even resistance to diseases. These traits can be **physical** (like skin color), **biochemical** (like blood type), or **behavioral** (like instinctive behaviors).
     
     - **Genes** – Genes are **specific segments of DNA** that contain instructions for making proteins, which determine the traits of an organism. Each organism has thousands of genes located on chromosomes.
    
- **DNA** is a double-stranded molecule that stores genetic information, using **A, T, G, C** bases, and is found mainly in the nucleus. It replicates to pass genetic information.

- **RNA** is a single-stranded molecule that helps in protein synthesis, using **A, U, G, C** bases. It carries genetic instructions (mRNA), forms ribosomes (rRNA), and transports amino acids (tRNA).

##### *GENES*

- **Sections of the DNA** structure that contain the **set of instructions** that determine the **characteristics or traits** of an organism are called *genes*.

- A **gene** is a specific sequence of **nucleotides** in DNA (or RNA in some viruses) that contains the instructions for making proteins or functional RNA molecules. 
- Genes are the **basic units of heredity**, meaning they are passed from parents to offspring and determine various traits, such as eye color, height, and even susceptibility to diseases. Genes are the basic structural and functional units of inheritance in nature

- Genes are like biological **blueprints** that tell cells how to build and regulate the body's structures and functions.
- A gene is like a **specific recipe** in the **DNA recipe book**, and it provides the instructions for making a specific **protein or functional RNA**.
    - A **gene has a starting point (promoter) and an endpoint (terminator)**, just like a recipe has a title and an end.


- The length of a gene varies greatly. Genes can be as short as **a few hundred base pairs** or as long as **millions of base pairs**.

    - The **smallest human gene** is **"HBB" (Hemoglobin beta chain)**, which is about **1,600 base pairs long**.
    - The **largest human gene** is **"DMD" (Dystrophin gene)**, which is about **2.3 million base pairs long**.

On average, human genes are **about 10,000–15,000 base pairs** long.

- The complete set of genes within a cell is known as the *genome*. 
- The full range of proteins that a cell is capable of producing is known as the *proteome*.

- A gene is not just a simple stretch of DNA; it has different functional parts:

    1. **Promoter Region** – A non-coding sequence at the beginning of the gene that acts as an "on/off" switch for gene expression. It signals where transcription should begin.
    2. **Exons** – The **coding** regions of the gene that contain instructions for making proteins. These are joined together during RNA processing.
    3. **Introns** – The **non-coding** regions that are removed before the final protein is made.
    4. **Terminator Region** – A sequence that marks the end of a gene, signaling when transcription should stop.

### *Functions of DNA*

- The function of DNA is to store all of the **genetic information** that an organism needs to grow,develop, reproduce, control the cell and survive. 
- While DNA determines the characteristics of an organism, it is also responsible for carrying and transmitting the hereditary materials or the genetic instructions from the parent to the offspring
- The transmission of this information from the mother to daughter cells occurs through the process of **DNA replication** during cell division.

### *DNA replication* 

- DNA replication is the **biological process** by which a cell makes an exact copy of its DNA before **cell division**.

- DNA is copied via a process known as **semi-conservative replication**. which means that each strand in the DNA double helix acts as a template for the synthesis of a new, complementary strand. In other words, the two original DNA strands separate during replication; each strand then serves as a template for a new DNA strand. Each newly synthesized double helix is a combination of one old and one new DNA strand
 


1. Before replication can start, the double strands of DNA need to separate from each other. An enzyme called **DNA helicase** breaks the [hydrogen bonds](https://www.expii.com/t/biology-hydrogen-bonds-10351)that connect the two strands, allowing them to come apart. The hydrogen bonds between complementary bases are broken. This unwinds the double helix and separates the strands.
    - The point where this unzipping starts is called an *origin point*
    - The points where DNA is currently unwinding are called *replication forks*.
    
![[Pasted image 20250217153103.png]]
2. The main enzyme involved in DNA synthesis is **DNA polymerase**. An enzyme called **RNA primase** lays down a *primer* on each template strand so the polymerase knows where to begin. Then, **DNA polymerase** moves down each strand and connects the complementary nucleotides, following the DNA sequences, to create the second complementary strand.
 -  But there is one issue with DNA polymerase—it can only move along the DNA strand in one direction, from the 3' end to the 5' end.
   So, when the DNA polymerase runs down a single strand of DNA, it moves from the 3' end to the 5' end, and it creates the second strand in the 5' to 3' direction. Each strand acts as a template as activated nucleotides attach to their complementary bases. Adenine pairs with thymine, while cytosine pairs with guanine.
![[Pasted image 20250217154957.png]]
>[!important] #### Leading Vs. Lagging Strands
>- The issue with this directionality thing is that DNA polymerases on each of the DNA strands will move in opposite directions. When it moves towards the replication fork, the strand it's on is called the **leading strand**. When it moves away from the replication fork (towards the origin), it's on the **lagging strand**.
> ![[Pasted image 20250217155302.png]]


3.  Replication along the **leading strand** is **continuous**.
 - but on the **lagging strand**, DNA polymerase has to make the new strand in segments called **Okazaki fragments**. Then, **DNA polymerase(delta)** replaces the RNA primers with DNA nucleotides, and an enzyme called **DNA ligase** has to connect all the fragments to create a continuous strand. 
 - The enzyme DNA polymerase joins the new nucleotides via phosphodiester bonds in the 5' to 3' direction in the leading strand . This forms the sugar-phosphate backbone of the new DNA strand. The enzyme DNA ligase is also used to join Okazaki fragments. 
 
![[Pasted image 20250217153231.png]]
4. Two identical copies of DNA are made. Each copy is made up of one original DNA strand and one new DNA strand (semi-conservative replication).
![[Pasted image 20250217153310.png]]
- the main enzymes evolved in the DNA replication process are: 

|**No**|**Enzyme**|**Function**|
|---|---|---|
|**1**|**DNA Helicase**|Unwinds the double-stranded DNA by breaking hydrogen bonds between base pairs, creating two single strands for replication.|
|**2**|**DNA Polymerase**|Adds new nucleotides to the growing DNA strand, ensuring proper base pairing, and polymerizes DNA using the template strand. Also plays a role in proofreading and error correction.|
|**3**|**RNA Primase**|Synthesizes short RNA primers complementary to the DNA strand to provide a starting point for DNA Polymerase.|
|**4**|**DNA Ligase**|Joins DNA fragments (such as Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand) by forming phosphodiester bonds, ensuring a continuous DNA strand.|
|**5**|**Topoisomerase**|Relieves the tension and prevents supercoiling ahead of the replication fork by making temporary cuts in the DNA strands.|

## **structure and function of RNA**

#### *structure of RNA*

- *Ribonucleic acid (RNA)* is a type of nucleic acid that uses information from DNA to synthesise proteins.
![[Pasted image 20250217175411.png]]
- **Each RNA nucleotide is made up of three components:** 

    1. **Ribose** - A pentose sugar.
    2. **A, U, G, or C base** - Adenine, uracil, guanine, or cytosine. 
    3. **A phosphate group**

- Unlike DNA, RNA nucleotides contain the **sugar ribose** rather than **deoxyribose**. They also contain the base *uracil* in place of *thymine* (uracil pairs with adenine).
![[rna-structure-illustration.png]]

RNA is a single stranded molecule made up of just one polynucleotide strand. These strands are much shorter than DNA strands.


![Table comparing DNA and RNA with details on pentose sugar, bases, size, number of strands, and diagrams of their structures.](https://s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/elements.cognitoedu.org/e248f23c-0a93-4793-acce-a632c3da06b8/comparing-dna-and-rna.png)

- There are three most well-known types of RNA in all organisms. These are **messenger RNA (mRNA),** **transfer RNA (tRNA)** and **ribosomal RNA (rRNA).** 
- All types of RNAs are formed on DNA strands by transcription process.
#### *Function of RNA*

- RNA is the most important molecule in all lives. RNA is involved in a variety of functions within the cell and is found in all living organisms. 
- RNA functions in protein synthesis and used as a storage of genetic information in some viruses.
- RNA facilitates the translation of the DNA into different proteins required by organisms. For example, it serves as a messenger in conveying instructions between the DNA and the ribosome during proteins synthesis.


## **[[Cell reproduction]]**

- **cell cycle** is a _sequence_ of _events_ that takes _place_ in the **parent cell** as a _means_ of _distributing_ _genetic material_ thereby forming a **daughter cell**.

- The cell cycle is an ordered series of events that involve cell growth and cell division to produce new daughter cells

- Organisms need new cells to grow and repair tissues in the body. Not all cells in the body are capable of dividing, but those that are follow a sequence of steps known as the **cell cycle.**

- **The cell cycle consists of three main phases:** 
     1. **Interphase** - This occupies the majority of the cycle as cells prepare for division.
     2. **Mitotic phase(karyokinesis)** - This is when the nucleus of the cell divides in two or more daughter cells. 
     3. **Cytokinesis** - This is when the whole cell and cytoplasm divides in two to produce two identical cells.

![[Pasted image 20250217184037.png]]
### *Interphase(preparatory phase)*

- During **interphase**, cells carry out their usual functions whilst also **preparing for cell division**. This involves replicating the DNA and organelles.
- it is the period of preparation for a cell to divide and start the cell cycle
-  It is the longest part of the cycle and can be thought of as the parent cell preparing for division.


- **Interphase consists of three phases:** 

1. **G1 phase** - This is when the cell grows and makes new proteins to replicate organelles.
2. **S phase** - This is when DNA is replicated. 
3. **G2 phase** - This is when the cell continues to grow and replicated DNA is checked for errors.
![[Pasted image 20250217185517.png]]
#### *G1 phase(first Gap phase)*

- *G1 phase (first gap)* is the first stage of interphase in which the cell is quite active at the biochemical level and The cell is **preparing** for DNA replication by **gathering resources** and **growing** in size.

- They accumulate the _building block_ of **chromosomal DNA or free nucleotide**, The cell **collects nucleotides** (A, T, C, G) to use later for making a copy of its DNA in the next phase (S phase)
- they associate **proteins** These are **histone proteins** that help **package DNA** into a compact structure called **chromatin**, meaning they **collects histone proteins**, which will later be used to wrap and organize DNA properly when it is replicated.
- they _store significant energy_ _reserves_ The cell needs **a lot of energy** for DNA replication and growth. This energy is mainly stored in **ATP molecules to complete task of replicating DNA**.
- All these preparations (nucleotide collection, histone proteins, and energy storage) help the cell **successfully copy its DNA** in the next phase.

- It is also the **Checkpoint** for the cell to determine whether it is ready to proceed to **S phase**
- If the cell is not ready, it can exit the cell cycle and enter a phase called **G0**.
- *The G0 phase (resting phase)*, is a phase in which the cell is neither dividing nor preparing to divide but performs regulatory and basic cellular functions.

#### *S phase (synthesis of DNA phase)*

- The S phase is a stage in which [[#*DNA replication*|DNA replication]] proceeds to form identical pairs of DNA molecules (*sister chromatids*) that are firmly attached to the *centromeric region*
- After DNA replication is complete, the chromosomes are made of identical **sister chromatids**. Each chromatid contains a double helix of DNA bundled up tightly. The two chromatids are held together by a centromere in the middle.

#### *G2 phase(second gap phase)*

- The **G2 phase** is a stage in which, the cell **replenishes its energy** stores that exhausted during DNA replication at S-phase.
- The cell **checks and finalizes preparations** for mitosis by producing necessary proteins, duplicating organelles, and reorganizing its internal structure.

- **synthesizes proteins** necessary for chromosome manipulation These are **special proteins** (such as *tubulin(spindle fibers)*) that help move chromosomes during mitosis. 
- In this phase, some cell organelles duplicate, The cell **copies some of its organelles** so that each of the two new daughter cells will have the necessary structures after mitosis.
- and the cytoskeleton(A network of **protein fibers** that gives the cell **shape and support** and helps in **cell movement**) disintegrates, The **cytoskeleton breaks down** so that its proteins (like tubulin) can be **used to form spindle fibers**, which help **move chromosomes** during mitosis.
- Cells might continue growing in this phase.

- After completing the interphase, the cell undergoes either mitotic or meiotic cell divisions.

### *Mitosis*

- [[Cell reproduction#**Cell Division**|mitosis]] is a type of cell division in which a parent cell divides to produce two **genetically identical daughter cells**. Each of these daughter cell contains the same DNA as the original parent cell.
- **Mitosis** is the division of *somatic cells*. Somatic cells make up most of your body's tissues and organs, including skin, muscles, lungs, gut, and hair cells

- Mitosis undergoes multistep processes during which a cell duplicates all of its contents, including its chromosomes and organelles, which the duplicated chromosomes are aligned, separated, and moved into respective poles then, two new identical daughter cells produced.

- **Cell division** consists of two main **sub-divisions**:

1. **Nuclear division** (**karyokinesis**).
    
    - results in the separation of **genetic** material of mother **cell** into _daughter cells_ by mitosis or _meiosis_.
2. **Cytoplasmic division** (**cytokinesis**).
    
    - it is the separation of the **cytoplasmic** components of the mother **cell** into the _daughter cells_


- Mitosis is one continuous process, but is often described as a **series of four stages**.

![[Pasted image 20250217193753.png|600]]

#### *Prophase*

- **Prophase is the first step in mitotic cell division** in which the nuclear envelope or nuclear membrane starts to dissociate into small vesicles

- The chromosomes condense (become shorter and thicker) and are now visible under a microscope.

- *centrosomes* migrate to opposite poles (sides) of the cell. Each **centrosome** starts forming **spindle fibres** from there **Centroles**.  

- The *nucleolus disappears* and the nuclear membrane starts to break down, leaving the chromosomes free in the cytoplasm, and the whole process takes 1 hour.

![[Pasted image 20250217195838.png]]
#### *Metaphase*

- **Metaphase** is the second step in the mitosis process. All the chromosomes are aligned at the **metaphase plate or the equatorial plane**, which is at the middle of the cell between the two poles of the cell.

- The chromosomes line up at the equator (middle) of the cell.
- Each chromosome attaches to the **spindle fibers** by their **Kinetochore** on the centromere. 

- At the metaphase checkpoint, each chromosome is checked to ensure it is attached to the spindle.
- this phase takes about 10min.
![[Pasted image 20250217195935.png]]
#### *Anaphase*

- During this phase, **cohesion proteins** linking similar **chromatids** together called the **centromere** breakdown.

- The **centromeres** divide to separate each pair of sister chromatids. 

- The *spindle fibres* contract and shorten(depolymerize) to pull the chromatids to opposite poles of the cell. 

- Each chromatid is pulled by its centromere, causing them to take on a 'V' shape when viewed under a microscope.
- this takes abouts 20min

![[Pasted image 20250217200456.png]]
#### *Telophase*

- The chromatids reach the opposite poles of the cell where they uncoil to become long and thin chromosomes again, **chromatids decondense back into chromatin**. 

- A nuclear envelope forms around each set of chromosomes to form two nuclei and the nucleolus starts to reform.

- The **mitotic spindles are depolymerized**, the nuclear envelopes form around the chromosomes and centrosomes appear within the nuclear area
- this takes about 30min
![[Pasted image 20250217200936.png]]
### *Cytokinesis*

- After the steps of *mitosis*, the cytoplasm divides in a process known as **cytokinesis**. This produces two daughter cells that are genetically identical to one another.
- the **Completion** in the **animal** **cells** is different from the **plant** **cell**.

    - as **animal** **cells** are surrounded only by **cell** **membranes**, **cytokinesis** enables the **cytoplasm** of the **mother** **cell** to pinch or contact. In the **middle**, as a result, two **daughter** **cells** completely separate.

    - as **plant** **cells** are surrounded by a **cell** **wall**, in addition to the **cell** **membrane**, the **cellulose** cannot easily pinch off and fully separate. Instead, a new **wall** will be laid down Between the two **daughter** **cells** called **middle** **lamella**.




### *Meiosis*

- **Meiosis** is a type of cell division in which a parent cell divides to **form four haploid cells**, each *genetically distinct* from one another.
- it is a type of **karyokinesis** in which the sex cells divide into four genetically distinct daughter cells.
- Meiosis involves a **reduction division** in which the **chromosome number is halved to form haploid cells**. These haploid cells form the *gametes (egg and sperm cells)* in animals and plants and creates genetic variation through **crossing over and independent assortment** .

- **The cell then undergoes 2 divisions:** 

    1. **Meiosis I** -  a cell duplicates all of its contents and divides into two daughter cells
    2. **Meiosis II** -  it divides into four different daughter cells with haploid number of chromosomes


- it has **eight stages**, four for each meiosis, **The two divisions of meiosis contain the same stages as mitosis:** 

    1. **Meiosis I** - Prophase I, metaphase I, anaphase I, and telophase I. 
    2. **Meiosis II** - Prophase II, metaphase II, anaphase II, and telophase II. 

- Remember that cells have already replicated their DNA during interphase and each chromosome is made up of two identical sister chromatids.

    - A **sex cell precursor** (the cell that will undergo meiosis) is **diploid (2n = 46 chromatin)**.
    - It contains **23 pairs of homologous chromatin** (one from the mother, one from the father).
    - At this stage, the chromatin is **not yet replicated**.

    - **After S-Phase (DNA Replication) in a Sex Cell Precursor** Each **chromatin duplicates**, forming **two identical sister chromatids** connected at the centromere.
    - Now, we have **46 chromosomes (X-shaped)**, but each **chromosome** consists of **two sister chromatids** (so there are **92 chromatids total)**.

    - **Meiosis I** Homologous **chromosomes** separate, reducing the number of chromosomes by half.
    - Each new cell now has **23 chromosomes** (each still made of **two sister chromatids**).
    - **Meiosis II** Sister chromatids separate.
    - Each gamete (sperm or egg) now has **23 chromatin (not connected anymore)**.

#### *Meiosis I*

- In diploid organisms, chromosome exists in pairs each members of the pair are called *homologous chromosomes*.

    - **Homologous chromosomes** are **pairs of chromosomes** that have the **same size, shape, and gene arrangement** but may carry **different versions (alleles) of genes**.
    - One chromosome in each pair comes from the **mother (maternal chromosome)** and the other from the **father (paternal chromosome).**
    - **Humans have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs), meaning 23 homologous pairs.**
##### *prophase I*
