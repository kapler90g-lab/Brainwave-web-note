## **Nucleic Acid**

- Nucleic acids are **complex biomolecules** essential for storing, transmitting, and expressing genetic information in all living organisms. 
- They are **polymers of nucleotides or polynucleotides** which consist of phosphoric acids, sugars and Organic bases and are primarily responsible for encoding the instructions needed for the growth, development, and reproduction of cells.
- nucleic acids are *polynucleotides* meaning they are polymers made of monomers called **nucleotides**, which are the building block of DNA and RNA

 - The two main types of nucleic acids are:
    1. **Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA)** – Stores genetic information.
    2. **Ribonucleic Acid (RNA)** – Helps in protein synthesis


- Nucleic acids are composed of three fundamental chemical components:
     - **Phosphoric Acid (Phosphate Group - PO₄³⁻)**
     - **Pentose Sugar**
     - **Organic Nitrogenous Bases**

#### **1. Phosphate Group (PO₄³⁻)**

- The phosphate group in DNA is an important part of the **nucleotide** structure. It consists of a **phosphorus atom** bonded to **four oxygen atoms**, making it negatively charged.
![[Pasted image 20250212133143.png|300]]
- This group is critical in forming the **backbone** of DNA when it links with the sugar molecules through a **phosphodiester bond** (a covalent bond between the phosphate group and the sugar’s hydroxyl group).

In the DNA strand, the **phosphate group** is located at the **5' end** of each nucleotide. The phosphate group of one nucleotide connects to the **3' hydroxyl group** of the sugar of the adjacent nucleotide.


#### **2. Pentose Sugar**

- The sugar in DNA is **deoxyribose**, a five-carbon sugar that differs from **ribose** (the sugar in RNA) by having one less oxygen atom at the 2' carbon (hence the name "deoxy"). This gives DNA its unique structure.

- Deoxyribose forms the **backbone** of DNA, alternating with phosphate groups to form the structure of the **double helix**.
- **Chemical structure:** $$C₅H₁₀O₄(Deoxyribose) \quad and \quad C₅H₁₀O₅(ribose)$$

![[Pasted image 20250212135456.png]]

- It has five carbon atoms:
    - **C1**: bonds with the nitrogenous base.
    - **C2**: the key difference between RNA and DNA, it has a hydrogen atom instead of a hydroxyl group in DNA and a hydroxyl group instead of a hydrogen atom in RNA (–H vs –OH).
    - **C3**: forms the bond to the next phosphate group.
    - **C5**: attaches to the phosphate group.

#### **3. Nitrogenous Bases**  
The nitrogenous bases are **organic molecules** that contain nitrogen and form the **coding sequences** in DNA. There are five bases:  
- **Adenine (A)** – Purine  
- **Thymine (T)** – Pyrimidine  
- **Guanine (G)** – Purine  
- **Cytosine (C)** – Pyrimidine

##### **Chemical Structures of the Bases:**

1. **Adenine (A)**  $\text{C₅H₅N₅}$
   - Adenine is a **purine** base, which means it has a **double-ring** structure.  

2. **Thymine (T)**  $\text{C₅H₆N₂O₂}$
   - Thymine is a **pyrimidine** base, meaning it has a **single-ring** structure.  

3. **Guanine (G)**  $\text{C₅H₅N₅O}$
   - Guanine is also a **purine** base, with a **double-ring** structure.  

4. **Cytosine (C)**  $\text{C₄H₅N₃O}$
   - Cytosine is a **pyrimidine** base, with a **single-ring** structure.

##### **Base Pairing(Chargaff's rule):**
In **DNA**, nitrogenous bases **pair specifically** due to their hydrogen bonding:
- **Adenine (A)** pairs with **Thymine (T)** (two hydrogen bonds).
- **Guanine (G)** pairs with **Cytosine (C)** (three hydrogen bonds).

These pairs form the **steps of the DNA ladder**, while the sugar-phosphate backbone forms the **sides of the ladder**.

#### **Building the DNA Double Helix:**

The DNA double helix is constructed by linking many **nucleotides** together. Here’s how this happens step-by-step:

##### **1. Formation of a Polynucleotide Chain (Single Strand):**

- **Phosphate-sugar backbone**: In a DNA strand, the **phosphate group** is attached to the **5' carbon** of the deoxyribose sugar in each nucleotide. When nucleotides link together to form a strand, the **phosphate group** of one nucleotide forms a **phosphodiester bond** with the **3' hydroxyl (-OH) group** of the sugar of the adjacent nucleotide. This results in the characteristic **5' to 3' directionality** of DNA strands.

- **Nucleotides** are joined together via **condensation reactions** to form a polynucleotide. The phosphate group of one nucleotide forms a covalent bond with the sugar of another. This forms a **phosphodiester bond**.

![[Pasted image 20250212142803.png]]

- **Nitrogenous base pairing**: The **nitrogenous bases** (A, T, G, C) of each nucleotide on one strand pair with the complementary bases (T, A, C, G) on the other strand.
- The two strands are **antiparallel**, meaning one runs **5' to 3'** and the other runs **3' to 5'**.
![[Pasted image 20250217091547.png|300]]
##### **2. Formation of the Double Helix:**

- The two strands of **polynucleotides** twist around each other to form the iconic **double helix**.
- The **base pairs** are held together by **hydrogen bonds**, with **A-T pairs** having two hydrogen bonds and **G-C pairs** having three.
- The twisting creates a **right-handed helix**, meaning the spiral goes clockwise.
