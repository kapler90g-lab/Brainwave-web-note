## Classification

### Principles of Classification:

**Classification** - is grouping similar living things together.

**Biodiversity** - is the great variety of life.

**Species** - A group of organisms that can breed and form organisms.

Living things are classified based on similarities and differences

- **pentadactyl - Five limbs**

**Morphology** - A branch of biology that studies the appearance of organisms.

**Taxonomy** - The process of classifying living organisms.

**Taxa** - The system of categorizing (classifying) organisms.

The main taxonomic classification are:
- Kingdoms
- Phylum
- Classes
- Order
- Family
- Genus
- Species

- The largest unit of classification is **Kingdoms**.
- The smallest unit of classification is **Species**.
- Kingdoms are classified into **Phylum**. Each *Phylum* into **Classes**. Each *Class* into **Orders**. Each *Order* into **Families**. Each *Family* into **Genus**. Each *Genus* into **Species**.

The naming of organisms was first introduced by **Carl Linnaeus**.

Based on **binomial naming system** - Which means having two names.

Biologists have developed a special way of identifying (classifying) organisms known as **biological or identification key**.

The well-known identification key is **dichotomous key**.

### The five kingdoms:

Kingdoms are the largest taxon and consist of all other taxa.

The five kingdoms are:
- Monera
- Protista
- Fungi
- Plantae
- Animalia

The System of Classification is known as _Five-Kingdom Classification_.





### **Kingdom Monera**
- Includes all the micro-organisms that don't have a nucleus (**Prokaryotes**).
- _Example_: all bacteria and 
  - blue-green algae.

### **Kingdom Protista**
- Includes all the microorganisms that *does have* a nucleus (*Eukaryotes*).
- _Example_: **Dinoflagellates** - Protista that cause bioluminescence, usually makes water bodies greener.

## **Kingdom Fungi**
- are eukaryotic and multicellular (some are unicellular _like yeast_).
- They are heterotrophic (about **80,000**).
- Many fungi are **saprotrophs**: meaning they feed on dead bodies.
- Fungi are parasites, feeding on living things; they mostly attack _plants more than animals_ (some attack animals including humans *like Candida albicans* (thrush) and _Tinea pedis (Athlete's foot)_.
- Some fungi are **mutualists**, meaning they live in an association in which both benefit.
  - _Example_: **lichens** (association between fungi and blue-green algae),
  **mycorrhiza** (association between fungi and plant roots).
  

# **Kingdom Plantae**
- So far there are **300,000** plant species.
- The main characteristics of plants are:
  - They have eukaryotic cells,
  - They are multicellular,
  - They contain chlorophyll and carry out photosynthesis,
  - They are autotrophic,
  - They are land-dwelling,
  - Most have a waxy cuticle that helps them from drying out.
- Plants divided into 4 Phylums. These are:
  1. **Bryophyta** (_mosses and liverworts_)
  2. **Pteridophyta** (_ferns_)
  3. **Gymnospermae** (_conifers_)
  4. **Angiospermae** (_true flowering plants_)

## Classes of Bryophyta (Mosses and Liverworts)

- *are the simplest land plants.*
- *They do not have true root and stem.*
- **They are non-vascular (they don't carry xylem (water and mineral) and phloem (food).)**
- *The largest species are less than 60 cm.*
- *They are commonly found in shady, damp places usually in the rainforest.*
- **Best Examples: Ceratodon, Funaria, spp.**
- *They have small, thin leaves.*
- *most have simple root-like structures called **rhizoids**.*
- **The other examples of Bryophyta is liverworts.**
    - *which only grow in wet places.*
    - *They are commonly found in high altitude and in rainforests.*

## Classes of Pteridophyta (Ferns)

- *They have true root and leaves and stems.*
- *Fern stems are **rhizome**s which grow horizontally below the soil.*
- *They have vascular bundles.*
- **They form spores which are dispersed by the wind to reproduce.**
- *most ferns live in damp/shady places.*
- *They grow in tropical rainforests.*
- *Some ferns need full sunlight to grow. Examples like Psilotum spp.*
- *The next plant division is the most successful in the kingdom of plants those are:
## Spermatophyta

- **They are successful B/C:**
  - *They have well-developed root, stem, and leaves.*
  - *They have well-developed vascular bundles.*
  - **The male gametes are contained in the pollen grains and the female gametes  in the embryonic sac.**
  - *They reproduce sexually.*
  - *Spermatophyta are divided into two classes:*
    - **gymnosperm**
    - **angiosperm**

### Orders of Gymnosperm

- *They are also known as Conifers (naked seeds).*
- *about 1/3 of the world's forest is conifer.*
- *They grow in cold-dominant region.*
- **Characteristics of Gymnosperm:**
    - **They have naked seeds.**
    - **They have small needle-shaped leaves with a sticky waxy cuticle.**
    - *They are evergreen.*
    - **They use cones as their reproductive structure.**
    - *There are two types of cones - male cones and female cones.*

### Orders of Angiosperm

- **Flowering Plants** are the largest groups of *plants*.  
- **Flowers** are their **reproductive**  *structure*.  
- The biggest **flower** in the world is - *Rafflesia arnoldii* (*1 meter across*).  
- The smallest **flowers** are **Wolffia globosa** (*less than 1mm across*).  
- The main **characteristics** are:  
    - **Flower** as their **reproductive** *structure*.  
    - They have **enclosed seeds** by *fruits*.  
    - They have **well-developed** *xylem and phloem*.

- The two main **order of** *angiosperms* are:  
  - **Monocotyledon** (*monocots*).  
  - **Dicotyledons** (*dicots*).

**Monocots**
- Their **embryo** has a **single seed**-*leaf*.  
- Leaves are longer and thin with *parallel veins*.  
- Contain **stem-scattered vascular bundles**.  
- Do not reach *great size*.  
- They are often **wind-pollinated**.  
- *Examples*: **Grasses**, **Grain** and **Maize**, and most of the *cereals*.

**Dicots**
- Have two **seed-leafs**.  
- Leaves are *broad and networked veins*.  
- Stem contains **rings of vascular bundles**.  
- *Mean great size*.  
- They are often **insect-pollinated**.  
- *Examples*: **Sunflowers**, **Peas**, **Roses**, and **Beans**.

### **Kingdom Animalia**  
- There are about *two million species*.  
- They are *multicellular*.  
- They are *heterotrophic*.  
- They exhibit *locomotion*.  
- They don't have *cell walls*.  
- There are *33 animal phyla*.  
	  1. **Porifera** (*sponges*).  
	  2. **Cnidaria**.  
	  3. **Platyhelminthes**.
	  4. **Nematoda**.
	  5. **Annelida**.
	  6. **Mollusca**.
	  7. **Echinodermata**.
	  8. **Arthropoda**.
	  9. **Chordata**.

## Phylum porifera (sponges)

- **Sponges** are the *simplest* *invertebrates*.
- Body *cavity* is connected to its external environment by **pores**.
- They are supported by **calcareous spicules**.

## Phylum Coelenterata

- They have soft bodies with **ring** of **tentacles** for capturing *prey*.
- Coelenterata have two *layers* of cells in their bodies that surround *central cavity*.
- They have *only one* *opening*, the mouth.
- Their bodies are **radial symmetry**.

## Phylum Platyhelminthes (flat worms)

- They show a high level of *organization*.
- Range from *2mm* to *30cm*.
- They possess a **front end** where the **mouth**, *sense organs*, the main integrating region found.
- They have *flattened* bodies with a **mouth** but no **anus**.
- They have *Body cavity* and rely on *diffusion* for everything.
- They are **hermaphrodites** - means they contain both male and female sex organs.
- They live both as a *parasite* (in humans) and *free-living* (in fresh waters).

## Phylum Nematoda (round worms)

- Are thought to be the *numerous* *animals* in the world.
- There are about 5 million in a top 10cm of an acre of soil.
- Have a **narrow thread-like** like bodies which are pointed at both ends and symmetrical.
- Their body is not segmented and are round in a cross-section.
- They don't have a *circulatory system* but they have a **complete digestive system**.

## Phylum Annelida (segmented or earth worms)

- Their body is divided into **regular segments**.
- They have a *closed* **blood circulatory system**.
- They are **hermaphrodites**.
- They have a **bristle-like** structure called **chaetae**-to help them move.
- Most are *free-living*.

## Phylum Mollusca

- They are the most **intelligent** *invertebrate species*.
- **Octopus** and *squid* have *well-developed brains*.
- They show a wide **diversity**.
- They have a **soft** muscular foot with a soft body protected by the **shell**.
- Their bodies are divided into **heads**, *foot and visceral mass*.
* They are not segmented
* They breathe through **gills**.

## Phylum Echinodermata:

* Their skins contain many **spines**.
* They have mouths, a gut, and anus.
* They are all marine animals.
* Move around using **tube feet**.
* Brightly-colored **sea cucumber** known as a **sea apple**.

## Phylum Arthropoda:

* Its name comes from Greek **Arthro**-joint, and **poda**-foot.
* They are the most varied animals on the Earth.
* They cannot grow very large.
* They are animals with segmented bodies and jointed limbs.
* They have well developed nervous systems and a complete gut (from mouth to anus).
* It is divided into major classes according to the number of limbs, presence of antennae, and no. body parts. Includes:
  - *Insecta*
  - *Crustacea*
  - *Chilopoda*
  - *Arachnida*
  - *Diplopoda*

### Insecta:
* Live almost everywhere.
* They are divided into three: head, thorax, and abdomen.
* Insects includes- Flies, butterflies, moths, beetles, wasps, and bees, etc.
### **Crustacea**
  - are mainly aquatic.
* Vary in size from- small to large.
* Their body is divided into two parts- **Cephalothorax** (head and thorax fused together) and **the abdomen.**
* Protected by a tough covering called **Carapace**.
* Crustacea include- Daphnia, crab, prawn, shrimp, barnacle, water flea, etc.

## Phylum Chordata:

* Their name is derived from **Notochord**.
* **Notochord**. is a flexible rod-like structure made of cartilage.
* Common features of Chordate animals:
	- They have notochord.
	- _They have a hollow nerve cord._
	- _They have gill slits during early stages. They are later replaced by lungs and gills._
