## **scalars and vectors** 

> **physical quantities** are any physical phenomena that can quantitively and qualitatively be measured and described.

- all physical quantities can either be a **scalar** or a **vector** quantity.

> **scalars** meaning scales or numbers, are **physical quantities** that can completely be described by a magnitude(size) with an appropriate measurement of unit.

- scalars have two main essential parts; the **magnitude** and **measurement of unit**, the magnitude tells us the amount of the physical quantity and the measurement of unit tells us which specific physical quantity we are measuring.

   - for example, when ever we say 12kg of an object we are describing the magnitude, 12 in this case and the measurement of unit, kg in this case which indicates mass of the object.

thus, **scalar quantities** are physical quantities that can completely be described by a magnitude with an appropriate measurement of unit.

> **vectors** are physical quantities that can completely be described by both a **magnitude and a direction** with the appropriate measurement of unit.

- vectors have three main essential parts; the magnitude, the direction and the measurement of units,  magnitude tells us the amount of the physical quantity and the measurement of unit tells us which specific physical quantity we are measuring and the direction tells us the path in which the physical quantity is directed.

- For example, when saying 12N applied to an object in the north direction, we are indicating a force of 12 newtons directed on an object to the north. 

**vector quantities** are are physical quantities that can completely be described by both a **magnitude and a direction** with the appropriate measurement of unit.

## **representations of vectors** 

- vectors can be represented in two main ways:

    - algebraically 
    - geometrically 

- algebraically, vectors can be represented by using a bold face symbol or an arrow over a symbol. for example force can be represented algebraically by **$F$** or $\overrightarrow{F}$  , so important equations like $F=ma$ can be write as $\overrightarrow{F}=m\overrightarrow{a}$  as both force and acceleration are vectors.

- geometrically, vectors can be represented by using an **arrow or an arrow-tipped line segment** which can represent both the magnitude and the direction of the vector
    - the tips of the arrow are named the **tail and the head** of the arrow
    - the **length** of the arrow when drawn to scale represents the magnitude of the vector.
    - and the **head** of the arrow represents the direction of the vector.

## **vector addition and subtraction** 

### *additions of vectors*

- additions of scalars is a simple mathematical sum as they do not have direction affecting the operation, addition of $5kg$ to $3kg$ is equal to $8kg$, the same goes to any scalar physical quantity.
- But the addition of vectors is not as straightforward as the addition of scalars, we need to account the direction of the vector in addition to the magnitude.

  > note that in both vectors and scalars only the same physical quantities can be added for example, mass and temperature can not be added together as they are not the same physical quantities. the same goes for vectors.
  
  - **resultant vector** is a single vector whose effect is the same as the individual vectors acting on an object.
  - if two vectors $\overrightarrow{A}$ and $\overrightarrow{B}$ have a resultant $\overrightarrow{R}$ , this can be represented mathematically as 
$$ \overrightarrow{R} = \overrightarrow{A}+ \overrightarrow{B}$$
- which means that the vector $\overrightarrow{R}$ has the same overall effect as the individual vector $\overrightarrow{A}$ and $\overrightarrow{B}$.

### *subtractions of vectors*

- subtraction of a vector is an extension of addition of a vector. subtraction of a vector is simply taking the addition of a **negative of a vector** with another vector.

  > a **negative of a vector** is a vector that has the same magnitude but opposite direction to another vector. and is mostly represented by "-" to indicate the opposite direction for example the negative of a vector $\overrightarrow{A}$ is $\overrightarrow{-A}$ 
- so subtraction of a vector is the addition of a negative of a vector to another vector. mathematically
$$ \overrightarrow{R} = \overrightarrow{A}- \overrightarrow{B}$$
 $$\overrightarrow{R} = \overrightarrow{A}+ \overrightarrow{(-B})$$
 - the order of subtraction affects the result of the resultant.

> two vectors in 1D will have the **maximum magnitude** when they both are in the **same direction** and have a **minimum magnitude** when they are in the exactly **opposite direction**.

## **Graphical method of vector addition**

- as we have mentioned previously vectors are graphically represented by using an arrow and the arrows can be translated to hold different positions in space this will allow us to add vectors pointing in different directions by joining them head to tail or tail to tail. there are three methods to add vectors graphically:
    - **triangular method**
    - **parallelogram method**
    - **Polygonal method**

### *Triangular method of vector addition* 

- this method is used to add **two vectors** by translating the head of the first vector and joining it to the tail of the second vector then drawing a new vector from the tail of the first to the head of the second vector this new vector is the **resultant vector**. 
- this results in a triangular shape hence the name the **triangular method of vector addition**. it is also named head-to-tail method as the vectors are joined head to tail.

- consider two vectors $\overrightarrow{A}$ and vector $\overrightarrow{B}$ at arbitrary positions in space, to add those two vectors we translate vector $\overrightarrow{B}$ 's tail to the head of $\overrightarrow{A}$ then we draw a new vector $\overrightarrow{C}$ from the tail of $\overrightarrow{A}$ to the head of $\overrightarrow{B}$ as shown bellow:
  ![[Pasted image 20250722115105.png|300]]
$$ \overrightarrow{C} = \overrightarrow{A}+ \overrightarrow{B}$$
- vector addition is **commutative**:
$$  \overrightarrow{A}+ \overrightarrow{B} = \overrightarrow{B}+ \overrightarrow{A} $$
- the difference is that the vector that is translated is always written to the right of the plus sign.

- To combine vectors using the triangle method:
    - **Step 1:** link the vectors head-to-tail
    - **Step 2:** the resultant vector is formed by connecting the tail of the first vector to the head of the second vector

### *Parallelogram method of vector addition*

- we can also apply the parallelogram method to add **two vectors** by translating the tail of the first vector to the tail of the second vector which creates the sides of a parallelogram, the diagonal of the parallelogram created by the two vectors is the **resultant vector**.

- To combine vectors using the parallelogram method:
    - **Step 1:** link the vectors tail-to-tail
    - **Step 2:** complete the resulting parallelogram
    - **Step 3:** the resultant vector is the diagonal of the parallelogram
### *Polygonal method of vector addition*

- In this method we can add **more than two vectors** together by joining all the vectors head-to-tail consecutively until all the vectors have been joined by a head to tail arrangement then we create a new vector from the tail of the first vector to the head of the last vector giving us our **resultant vector**.

$$  \overrightarrow{R} = \overrightarrow{A}+ \overrightarrow{B} + \overrightarrow{C}+ \overrightarrow{D} ....$$

- to combine vectors using our polygonal method:
     - **step 1:** link all the vectors head to tail
     - **step 2:** create a new vector from the tail of the first vector to the head of the last vector.



## **special cases of vector addition** 

### *parallel vectors*

- If two vectors are acting the same direction the magnitude of the resultant is equal to the sum of the magnitude of the two vectors.
$$ | \overrightarrow{R}| = |\overrightarrow{A} + \overrightarrow{B}| $$
- the direction of the resultant vector is the same as the direction of both of the vectors.

### *antiparallel vectors*

- If two vectors are exactly in the opposite direction the magnitude of the resultant is the difference between the two vectors.
  $$ | \overrightarrow{R}| = |\overrightarrow{A} - \overrightarrow{B}| $$
- the direction of the resultant is the same direction as the larger vector.

### *perpendicular vectors*

- if two vectors are perpendicular the magnitude of the resultant is obtained by using the **Pythagorean theorem**.
  $$ | \overrightarrow{R}| = \sqrt{|\overrightarrow{A}|^2 + |\overrightarrow{B}| ^2}$$
- the direction of the vector can be obtained by finding the angle of the resultant using trigonometry:
   $$ \theta = tan^{-1}(\frac{B}{A}) $$

## **Vector resolution**

- **resolving a vector** is the process of breaking down a resultant vector into its smaller individual vectors when added together give back the original resultant vector. the smaller individual vectors are known as **components**. 
- so the process of breaking down a vector into its components is known us **vector resolution**.
-  For example, a force vector of magnitude $F_R$ and an angle of _θ_ to the horizontal is shown below
![[Pasted image 20250722135006.png]]

- It is possible to **resolve** this vector into its **horizontal** and **vertical** components using trigonometry
![[Pasted image 20250722135111.png|500]]
- The direction of the resultant vector is found from the angle it makes with the horizontal or vertical
    
    - The question should imply which angle it is referring to (i.e. calculate the angle from the x-axis)
    
- Calculating the angle of this resultant vector from the horizontal or vertical can be done using **trigonometry**
    
    - Either the sine, cosine or tangent formula can be used depending on which vector magnitudes are calculated
    

- For the **horizontal** component, $\overrightarrow{F_x}=Fcos\theta$
- For the **vertical** component, $\overrightarrow{F_y}=Fsin\theta$

thus the original vector force can be written as the sum of its components:
$$ \overrightarrow{F}= \overrightarrow{F_x}+ \overrightarrow{F_y} $$
