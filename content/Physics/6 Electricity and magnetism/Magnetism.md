#grade10 #unit5 #magnatism
_**magnetism**_ is an attraction that allows certain kinds of objects called **magnets** to exert force on each other **without physically touching**.

## Magnet

- The use of **magnet** was discovered by the ancient Greeks during the Greek civilization.
- They found a stone that was able to attract certain kinds of objects called **lodestone**. It attracts objects like **iron** and **nickel**.
- A magnet is a material or an object that produces a **magnetic field** that is responsible for a force that pulls or attracts **other materials**.
- Magnets can attract objects made of **iron** or **steel**; they can also attract or repel other magnets.
- They are made of different shapes and create their own **persistent magnetic field**.
- They are used in various appliances to either change the electrical energy into mechanical energy or mechanical energy into electrical energy.

## Types of Magnets

There are three main categories of magnets:
- **Permanent magnet**
- **Temporary magnet**
- **Electromagnets**

### Permanent Magnets

Permanent magnets are made up of magnetic materials such as **steel** that is magnetized and has its own magnetic field. They are known as permanent magnets because they do not lose their magnetic properties once they are magnetized.

However, their strength depends on the nature of the material used in its creation.

**There are three main ways to demagnetize a permanent magnet:**

- **Exposing it to an extreme temp,**
- **By hammering them Because the magnetic attraction between the magnets atom get loose,**
- **Striking one magnet to another will reduce the magnetic strength.**

### Temporary Magnets (ferromagnets)

There are magnets created in the presence of magnetic fields when the field is removed those magnets lose their magnetic properties   

### Electromagnets

- They are magnets that consist of coils of wire wrapped around a metal core made of iron.
- When the coil of wire conducts a current, a magnetic field is generated, making the material behave like a magnet.
- And its strength can be controlled by controlling the electric current supplied.

## **Properties of a Magnet**

  - The ends of a magnet are known as **poles**.
  
  - Magnetic poles always exist in pairs.
  
  - When a magnet is suspended freely, it always points to the geographic north-south poles.
  
  - The pole pointing to the geo-north is called **North pole**.
  
  - The pole pointing to the geo-south is called **South pole**.
  
  - Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.

## magnetic field 

- **Magnetic Field** is a field produced by a magnetic material or by a moving electric charge.
- They are represented by using *magnetic field lines*.
- **Magnetic field lines** are a visual tool used to visualize the **strength** and **direction** of a magnetic field.

### The basic properties of a magnetic field

- **Magnetic fields** never cross each other.
- Magnetic field lines form a **closed loop**.
- **Outside a magnet**, magnetic field lines appear in the North Pole and terminate in the South.
- **Inside a magnet**, magnetic field lines appear South and terminate in North Pole.
- **Field line density**, which is the number of field lines per unit area, is directly proportional to the **strength** of the magnetic field.
- The magnetic field lines are stronger at the poles; the field lines are **denser** at the poles.

## Similarities *Between* Electric and Magnetic Fields

- Electric fields are created by two **types** of charges, 
  **positive** and **negative**, and are magnetic fields are associated with two magnetic poles They are also produced by a *moving* charge.  
- Just like charges, *like poles repel* while *unlike poles attract*.  
- Electric fields point in the **direction** of the forces experienced by a *positive charge*. Similarly, *magnetic fields* point in the direction of the force experienced by North pole.

## Differences *Between* Electric and Magnetic Fields

- Positive and negative charges *can exist separately*, while magnetic poles always *come in pairs*.  
- Electric field lines have a defined starting and end points, while magnetic field lines form a **continuous loop**, with no defined *starting* and *end points*.  

## *Earth's* Magnetic Field

- Also called the **Geomagnetic Field**, is a magnetic field that extends from the Earth's *interior* out into space and causes a compass needle to *rotate*.  
- The movement of the molten *iron core* is responsible for generating the *Earth's* magnetic field.  
- The shape of the *Earth's magnetic field* is similar to that of a huge bar magnet tilted about $11^\circ$ from the earth's **Geographical poles**.
- The earth has two sets of **North** and **South pole**.
    - **Geographical poles,**  
    - **Magnetic poles**

**Geographical poles** are poles that are two fixed points where the earth's axis of rotation intersects its surface.

**Magnetic poles** are poles unlike **Geographical poles**; they are not fixed and move with the molten outer core. Those are the poles that compasses point to.

### **Compass**  
is an instrument that is used to find the direction of a magnetic field. 
- In the presence of magnetic field, the needle is able to line up in the same direction as the field.
- Interestingly, the North Pole of the Compass points towards the earth's magnetic North Pole, which is actually a magnetic South pole.

## **Magnetic field of a Current-carrying Conductor**
- **Current** is generally defined as the rate of flow of charge.
- A stationary charge produces an electric field directly proportional to the magnitude of the charge.
-  a moving electric charge also produces a magnetic field, which is proportional to the current supplied.
- Hence, a current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field, this magnetic field is generally attributed to the subatomic particles in the conductor.

**Strength of the magnetic** field **B** with some distance $d$ from a current carrying conductor $I$ can e found using the equation: 
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi d} $$  
where $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \, \text{T m/A}$ refers to permeability of free space.

- This SI unit of a magnetic field **B** is **Tesla (T)**, after the brilliant inventor Nikola Tesla.
- Another smaller unit is called **Gauss (G)**; 1G = $10^{-4} T$.
- The strongest permanent magnet has a field of **2T**.
- The Earth's magnetic field on its surface is only about $5\times 10^{-5} \, T$ or **0.5G**.

- The distance **d** must be measured perpendicular to the wire.
- The strength of the magnetic field **B** decreases as the distance **d** from the wire increases, mathematically,

  $$ B \propto \frac{1}{2 \pi d} $$

**Proof**

1. **Biot-Savart law.**
   - States that the magnetic field **B** by a point in a current-carrying wire is given by:

   $$ \mathbf{dB} = \frac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \times \mathbf{I} \times \frac{(d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{r})}{r^3} $$

2. **Integrate,**

   $$ \mathbf{B} = \int \left( \frac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \right) \times \mathbf{I} \times \frac{(d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{r})}{r^3} $$

3. **Simplification.**

   $$ \mathbf{B} = \left( \frac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \right) \mathbf{(I)} \left( \int \frac{(d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{r})}{r^3} \right) $$

4. **Cross product simplification.**

   $$ d\mathbf{l} \times r = d\mathbf{l} \times r \times \sin \theta \quad (\theta = 90 \degree) $$

   $$ d\mathbf{l} \times r \times 1 $$

5. **Integration and distance,**

   $$ \mathbf{B} = \left( \frac{u_o \mathbf{I}}{4 \pi} \right) \left( \int \frac{d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{I}}{r^3} \right) $$
   
$$ \Rightarrow \left( \frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi r^2} \right) \times \frac{d}{d^3} $$

6. **Simplification** and **Final Equation**,

$$ \Rightarrow \frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi } \times \frac{1}{d^2} \Rightarrow \frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi d^2} = B $$

- Since **magnetic field** is a **vector quantity**, one needs to determine the direction of the magnetic field.  
- We **use** the **right hand rule** to determine the direction of the magnetic field.  

#### **Right Hand Rule:** (Fleming's RHR)

**It states that if you wrap your hand around the wire and point your thumb in the direction of the current the other finger will show the direction of the magnetic field around the wire.**  
- Generally speaking, the magnetic field produced due to a current-carrying conductor has the following characteristics:  
    - **It encloses the conductor.**  
    - **It is perpendicular to the conductor.**  
    - **A change in the direction of the current changes the direction of the field.**  

## **Magnetic force on a moving charge placed in a uniform magnetic field.**

- f a charged particle is in motion in a magnetic field with a velocity (V) in the magnetic field (B), then it will feel a force known as **magnetic force.**

- At the rest positions, these charged particles are unaffected by a magnetic field. But as they begin to move, they create a magnetic field; they get affected by the magnetic field of the magnet.

- **Uniform or steady magnetic field.**
    -  *Is a region of a field where it has equal magnitude of strength and same direction at each point.*

- *So a particle that is moving in a uniform magnetic field has a magnetic force of:*

  $$ F = qvB \sin \theta $$

**Proof**

1. **Lorentz force**
   - According to the Lorentz law, the magnetic field is
   $$ F = q (v \times B) $$

2. **Cross product**
    $$ v \times B = |v| |B| \sin \theta \times n $$

3. **Magnitude of the cross product**
    $$ |v \times B| = |v| |B| \sin \theta $$

4. **Substituting to the Lorentz equation.**

$$F = qvB\sin \theta$$

where **q** is the charge (C) , **$v sin \theta$** is the magnitude of the velocity, which is perpendicular to the direction of the magnetic field (T), **B** is the magnetic field (T).  

- The magnitude of the charge is zero when the direction of the magnetic field and the velocity of the particles are parallel to each other.  
- It reaches the maximum when they are full perpendicular to each other.  

- To find the perpendicular direction of the magnetic force we can use the **right-hand rule**.  

#### The Right-Hand Rule

- The direction of the magnetic force is perpendicular to the plane created by the **B** and **v**.  
When the thumb points to the **v** of the charge **q**, and the other four finger to the direction of the field, and the face of the palm points to the magnetic force.  

## **Magnetic Force on a Current Carrying Wire.**  

- A force on a current carrying wire is similar to that of a **moving charge** as **Expected**. Since a **charge-carrying wire** is a collection of moving charges and in the presence of a magnetic field it feels a force.  
These forces are transmitted to the material of the conductor. The conductor as a whole experiences a force distributed along its length.  
The force on a wire carrying a current $I$ with a length L in a uniform magnetic field B is given by 

$$ 
F = ILB \sin \theta 
$$

**Proof**  
1. **Lorentz Law**  
   $$ 
   F = q \times \vec{v} \times \vec{B} 
   $$  
2. **Current and Velocity**  
   $$ 
   I = \frac{q}{t} \quad V = \frac{L}{t} 
   $$  
3. **Substituting**  
   $$ 
   F = (It)\times(\frac{L}{t})\times B sin\theta 
   $$  
4. **Simplifying**  
   $$ 
   F = I LB \sin \theta 
   $$  

#### **Right Hand Rule**  
- States that the **force** is perpendicular to the **magnetic field** and **electric current**.
