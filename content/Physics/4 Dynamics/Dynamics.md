## Force

>**dynamics** is the study of the cause of motion

> **force** is a push or a pull applied on a body to change its state of motion

the magnitude of the force applied on a specific direction causes an object to change in state of motion.

thus forces are the main causes of motion and are a vector quantity

apart from changing the speed and direction or both of a body it can also change the shape and size of the object is known as **deformation**

depending on how forces are applied they can either be **contact forces** or **field forces**

- **contact forces** are forces that is applied by having a physical contact between bodies e.g. pushing a box, kicking a ball..etc
- while a **field force** is the force applied at a distance with no physical contact e.g. gravitational forces, electrostatic forces and magnetic forces are some to mention

All of the four fundamental forces are field forces those are:
- strong nuclear force
- electrostatic force
- weak nuclear force
- gravitational force

## Newton's laws of motion

sir Isaac Newton was the first person to ever formulate how forces bring about motion and the working of the heavenly bodies

he formulated three laws as follows:

### Newton's First law

This law starts by addressing the question what happens if there is no force applied to change a a state of motion?

**A state of motion** is of two types, it is either at rest also called stationary or it is at a constant uniform motion in straight line called an inertial motion.

when a force is applied it changes the state of the motion of the body as we discussed before but if no force is applied an objects state of motion will not change forever meaning an object at rest will remain at rest and an object at a uniform motion will continue in that state of motion.

so this law states that:

> an object at rest will remain at rest an object in motion will remain in motion unless an external force is acted upon it.

this law is also called the **law of inertia**, **inertia** is the property of an object to oppose a change in its state of motion.

and an object will higher mass has higher inertia as it requires a lot of force to change the state of the motion of the object and vice versa.

>thus mass is a measure of inertia.

motion is relative, the body by which a motion of another can be compared with is known as **reference frame**. there are two types of reference frame, an inertial frame of reference and a non-inertial frame of reference

- **an inertial frame of reference** is a reference where a body has either of two state of motion at rest or a constant velocity, this is where most Newtons laws hold

- a **non-inertial frame of reference** on the other had is when there is a constant change in the sates of the motion of an object

### Newton's second law

This law asks the opposite of the question asked at the first law it says what happens if there is a not force being applied on an object?

If a body was moving at a constant velocity and an external force was applied on it the state of motion changes and its velocity starts to change it starts to **accelerate**

thus the ultimate change that a force can cause on an object is to change its velocity or to accelerate it.

if a body is accelerating then there is a force applied and if there is a force applied there is acceleration.

if the force doubles the acceleration doubles and if the force triples the acceleration triples but if the force doesn't change but the mass does then if the mass doubles the acceleration halves, if the mass triples the acceleration will be one-third of its original value.

- thus the acceleration is directly proportional to the force applied and is inversely proportional to the mass

the Newton's second law states that:

> when unbalanced force is applied on an object of a mass m the object accelerates in the direction of the force applied and the magnitude of the acceleration is directly proportional to the force applied and is inversely proportional to the mass.

Mathematically,$$ a \propto {F_{\text{net}}}{} \ \text{and} \ \ a \propto \frac{1}{m}$$

thus, $$a = \frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m}$$

$$\boxed{F = ma}$$

the SI-unit of force is **Newton(N)** named after Sir Isaac Newton and can be expressed in forms of the fundamental units as:

$$ \text{1N} = 1 \text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}^2 $$

### Newton's third law

when a body exerts a force on a second body, the second body exerts a force equal in magnitude opposite in direction.

- the force the first body applied is called an **action force**
- and the reactive force that is applied by the second body on the first is called **a rection force**

action and reaction force always come in pair and are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction but doesn't result in a net zero force as each of them apply a force on different bodies, it is when a force that are equal and opposite act on the same body they cancel out.

thus newton third law states that:

> for every action force there is an equal and opposite reactive force

## Weight and Gravitational force

> thus the weight of a body is the gravitational force a body of a mass m experiences

the weight of a body is not an intrinsic property as it depends on the gravitational strength

### **4.2. Frictional Force**

- In simplest terms, it is a force that **opposes motion**.
- It is a type of **Contact Force**.
- It occurs when two surfaces slide or try to slide over each other.
- It occurs because of the roughness of the surfaces in contact.
- Even though some smooth-looking surfaces look smooth, they are not at the micro or nano scale.
![[Pasted image 20240915072454.jpg]]


- There are two types of friction:
  - **Static Friction**
  - **Kinetic Friction**

#### **Static Friction**

- It is the friction **on the surface where there is no movement**.
- This applies (it is a force which you have to overcome to start the movement which is often denoted by $F_s$) and is called **static friction**.
![[Pasted image 20240915072639.png|300]]

### Kinetic Friction

* **Kinetic Friction**: B/n two surfaces when one of them is sliding over the other.
* Denoted by $F_k$.
* It is related to the normal force $F_N$.
* And its value depends on the type of obj. in contact.
* The property of the material to resist motion is called: *Coefficient of friction* ($\mu$).
* And it is the ratio B/n the normal force and frictional force.

$$
\text{Coefficient of friction} = \frac{\text{Frictional Force}}{\text{Normal Force}} \Rightarrow \mu = \frac{F_F}{F_N}
$$

* So we write:
$$
F_s = \mu_s F_n \, \text{(for a static frictional force)} \, \Rightarrow \, F_s \le \mu_s F_N
$$
$$
F_k = \mu_k F_n \, \text{(for a kinetic frictional force)}
$$

* Kinetic friction is less than static friction.
  $$
  F_k < F_s \Rightarrow \mu_k < \mu_s
  $$

* Unlike the $F_k$, the $F_s$ can take on any value b/n zero and its maximum value $(\mu_s N)$.

### **4.2 The First Condition of Equilibrium**

- If an object is at rest or uniformly moving at a constant speed, it is said to be in **equilibrium**.
- If an object is at rest or constant speed in a fixed direction (**constant velocity**), then it is in **translational equilibrium**. This defines the first condition of equilibrium.
- If an object is in equilibrium, its acceleration is zero and according to Newton's second law, the vector sum of all the forces is zero.

$$\sum_{n=1}^N \vec{F}_n = 0 \Rightarrow \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 + \ldots + \vec{F}_N = 0$$

- Components also can be written as: 

$$\sum_{x=1}^X F_x = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{y=1}^Y F_y = 0.$$

### **work , Energy and Power**

#### **Work**

- It means the transfer of energy from one body to another.
- **Energy is the ability to do work and it cannot be created or destroyed**.

**Work done by a constant force:**
- Work, when some force is applied, in physics, is defined as the force applied to an object to move it in a given distance.
- The definition might be taken as:

  $$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{S}$$
  
  However, it gives only the magnitude when the force is parallel to the displacement.

- For a more general case, work is defined as the scalar product of the force and the displacement vectors.

$$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{S} \cos{\theta} \quad \text{where} \quad \theta \; \text{is the angle between} \; \vec{F} \; \text{and} \; \vec{S}$$
![[Pasted image 20240915072918.jpg|300]]

- A force acting at an angle can be written as:

  $$W = (F \cos{\theta})S$$

- $F \cos{\theta}$ is the force acting in the direction of the displacement, and $F \sin{\theta}$ is perpendicular to the displacement. So, there is no work in that direction.
![[Pasted image 20240915073046.png|300]]

 **Work done by the gravitational force** 
 
- If an object is thrown up with $\vec{s}$, the initial $\vec{s}$ will have an *initial kinetic energy* of $k_e = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.
- As the object moves up, it slows down by the **gravitational force** $\vec{F_g}$, meaning the *kinetic energy* decreases as gravitational force does work on the object.

$\text{The work done by } F_g$

- Since $F_g = ma$, we get:
  $$W_g = F_gs\cos\theta$$

- When $\theta = 180^\circ$, the work done is:
  $$
  W_g = mgs(\text{B/C} \cos 180^\circ = -1
  )$$
  (The “-” sign tells us that $F_g$ is opposite to $K.E.$)

- After the object reaches its max height, it is falling downwards, the angle is $0^\circ$ b/n F and the S, and the Thus, $W_g$ will be:
  $$
  W_g = mgs\cos 0^\circ = +mgs
  $$
  (The “+” sign tells us that $F_g$ and $K.E.$ are in the same direction. Thus, $mgs$ transforms energy to the form of $K.E.$)

### Kinetic Energy
- The energy possessed due to motion is known as *KE*.
- The general formula for *KE* is:
  $$
  KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
  $$
- The work done by a moving object is equal to the $\Delta K.E.$ 
- and this is called the $$W = \Delta K.E. = \frac{1}{2}mv_2^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_1^2$$ (*Work-Energy Theorem*).
- $$W = \Delta K.E$$

---

### Potential Energy

- The energy due to its relative position or shape is known as *U*.
- The energy due to some relative position is called *G.P.E* and the energy due to its shape is known as *E.P.E*.
- The GPE of a body of a given mass $m$ and height $h$ is given by:
  $$
  GPE = mgh
  $$
- And the EPE of a given body on an elastic spring of a portion $x$ is given by:
  $$
  EPE = \frac{1}{2}kx^2
  $$
  (where $k$ is the spring constant and depends on the nature of the body)

- The sum of the $KE$ and $PE$ is termed as the total mechanical energy of the body:
  $$
  ME = KE + PE
  $$

- The $\Delta GPE$ is defined as being equal to the *negative* *work done on the o by the gravitational force*.:
  $$
  \Delta U_g = -W
  $$

# Conservative and **non-Conservative** Forces

- Generally, there are **TWO TYPES OF FORCES**, namely:
  - Conservative forces, and   
  - Non-conservative forces.
  
- In **conservative forces**: 
  1. The work done by a conservative force of any two points is independent of the path taken by the particle.
  2. The work done by a conservative force on a particle moving through any closed path is zero.
    - **Example**: Gravitational force is a conservative force.

- A **force** is non-conservative if it doesn't satisfy properties 1 and 2 of conservative forces.
  - A **non-conservative force** causes changes in the mechanical energy of the body.
  - The work done by a non-conservative force is equal to the change in mechanical energy of the body.
    - **Example**: A typical non-conservative fore (also called a dissipative force) is frictional force.

- $W_f$ is the work done by dissipative forces:
  $$W_f = \Delta KE + \Delta U$$
  $$F_x = \Delta KE + \Delta U$$

- Where $F_x$ is the frictional force.

## Power

- **Power** is the rate of change of doing work.
- Meaning, work done during some time interval.

**Average Power** = $\frac{\text{Work done}}{\text{Time taken}}$

Symbolically,

$$P_{av} = \frac{W}{t}$$

- The SI Unit is **Watt**.
  - **1 Watt** = **1 J/s**
  - Another unit of power is horsepower.
    - **1 horsepower** = **746 watts**

**Another Expression for Power:**

- Using, $$W = F \cdot S$$, 
  $$P = \frac{W}{t} = \frac{F \cdot S}{t} = F \cdot v$$

So, $$P = F \cdot \upsilon$$

- **Instantaneous Power** can be written with **Instantaneous velocity**.

**Instantaneous Power** = Force x Instantaneous velocity:

$$P = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$$

- And it can also be termed as the dot scalar product of the force and velocity vectors.

# Conservation of _Mechanical Energy_

- The law of **Conservation of Energy** states that:
  - In the absence of dissipative forces (friction, air resistance, etc...) the total $ME$ of the object remains unchanged.
  $$
  \Delta E = 0
  $$
  $$
  \Delta KE + \Delta PE = 0
  $$
  $$
  (KE + PE) = (KE_0 + PE_0)
  $$

## **Impulse and Linear Momentum**

- **Linear momentum** is the property of a moving object that describes how hard it is to set a body in motion or to stop it.
- It is *hard* to stop a massive object than a light object given their speed is the same.
- Linear momentum is the mass multiplied by the velocity.

$$
\text{Linear momentum} = \text{mass} \times \text{velocity}
$$

$$
\mathbf{p} = m \mathbf{v} \quad \text{also} \quad \Delta \mathbf{p} = \Delta (m \mathbf{v})
$$

- It is a vector quantity directed along the direction of the velocity.
- And the SI unit is **Kg m/s**.
- Change in the momentum of a body requires the application of force according to Newton's **2nd law**: velocity of a moving object changes when a non-zero force acts on it.

$$
F = ma = m \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} \Rightarrow m \frac{v_f - v_i}{\Delta t} \Rightarrow \frac{P_2 - P_1}{\Delta t} \Rightarrow \frac{\Delta \mathbf{p}}{\Delta t} \quad \text{(Newton's 2nd law in terms of momentum)}
$$

- So the net force (vector sum of all the forces) is equal to the rate of change of momentum.
$$
F_{net} = \frac{\Delta \mathbf{p}}{\Delta t} = {\Delta \mathbf{p}}={F_{net} \Delta t}
$$

- The product of the net force and time interval, $F \Delta t$, is also known as **Impulse**.
- Denoted by $\overrightarrow{J}$
$$
J = F \Delta t
$$

- **Impulse-momentum theory**:

  - Say that the change in momentum of the body is equal to the impulse of the net force that acts on the period during the interval
  
  $$
  \Delta P = F_{net} \Delta t
  $$
  $$
  J = F_{net} \Delta t
  $$
  $$
  J = \Delta P
  $$

- So generally **Impulse** is the **Change in Momentum**

## Collisions

- When two particles come close to each other with contact by a force it is called **Collision**.
- Depending on the type of collision, **KE** may or may not be conserved.
- Collisions, depending on conservation of **KE** is classified as **Elastic** and **Inelastic collision**.
  - **Elastic collision** is the collision in which total momentum and **KE** is conserved.
  - **Inelastic collision** is the collision in which total momentum is conserved but **KE** is not.


## Center of Mass

- The point where the total mass of a body or system of objects is assumed to be concentrated.
- Suppose we have several particles with mass $m_1$, $m_2$, and so on.
- Let the coordinates of $m_i$ be $(x_{1}, y_{1})$, $M_2 (x_{2}, y_{2})$ and so on, we define the center of mass as:

$$ x_{\text{cm}} = \frac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2 + \ldots + m_n x_n}{m_1 + m_2 + \ldots + m_n} $$

$$ y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{m_1 y_1 + m_2 y_2 + \ldots + m_n y_n}{m_1 + m_2 + \ldots + m_n} $$
