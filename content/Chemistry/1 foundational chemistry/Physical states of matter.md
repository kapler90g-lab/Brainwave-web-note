## **introduction to physical states of matter**

- Substances exist in **different physical states**; for example, water (liquid), sugar (solid), and CO 2 (gaseous) states at room temperature because of the presence of **different intermolecular forces** among the particles of solids, liquids, and gaseous substances under given conditions.

- **Matter** is defined as anything *occupying space and having mass*, which is the material of the Universe. It exists in three common states: *gas, liquid and solid.*
- **Liquids and gases** are known as *fluids* because they **flow freely**, **Solids and liquids** are referred to as *condensed states* because they have much higher densities than gases
- the physical states of matter can be changed to solids, liquids, gases, or plasma by varying **temperature and/or pressure**.

### *Solid*

- a solid is a rigid body.
- it has a **fixed volume and a fixed shape**
- Solids cannot be compressed, they are **incompressible** 
- Solids have **higher densities** than liquids because the particles are tightly packed together with a **strong intermolecular forces.** 
- this tightness gives solids their organized shape.
- they are not **fluids** because they don't **flow freely.**

![[Pasted image 20250219085440.png|400]]

### *Liquid*
- Has a _definite volume_ but no _definite shape_ they take the shape of the container that they are in. 
- they are very slightly **compressible** and they can flow freely because they are **fluids** 
- In a liquid, particles *vibrate about a point*, and *constantly shift their positions*

![[Pasted image 20250219090932.png|400]]
### *Gas*
- Has neither a _definite shape_ nor _a definite volume_ it takes the shape and the volume of its container.
- Gas is **highly compressible** because most of the volume of a gas is composed of the large amounts of empty space between the gas particles.
- they are **fluids** 

![[Pasted image 20250219091521.png|400]]
### *Plasma*
- Plasma is the fourth physical state of matter that exists at a high temperature
- gaseous mixture of **positive ions and electrons**, is called *plasma*, in another word, *plasma is an ionized gas*
- like gases, plasmas have **no fixed shape and a fixed volume**. They are also **less dense** than solids or liquids
- **No material can exist in a plasma state.**

## **kinetic Theory and properties of matter**

### *Kinetic theory of matter*

- The three states of matter in which substances are **chemically the same** but **physically different**, is explained by the **Kinetic Theory of Matter**.
- Every substance consists of a very large number of very small particles called **ions, atoms, molecules**.
- The **motion of the particle increases** with **a rise in temperature**.

- Generally, the **kinetic theory of matter** is based on the following assumptions:

    1. All matter is composed of **particles(ions, atoms, molecules)** that are *constantly moving*.
    2. All particles have **kinetic energy (movement energy) and potential energy(energy of position)**. Molecules in the **solid phase** have the **least amount of energy**, while **gas particles** have the greatest **amount of energy**.
    3. The difference between the three states of matter is the **energy contents** they have and the **motion of the particles**. A change in phase may occur when the energy of the particles is changed.
    4. The **temperature** of a substance is a measure of the **average kinetic energy of the particles**.
    5. There are spaces between particles of matter. The **average amount of empty space** between molecules gets **progressively larger** as a sample of matter moves from the solid to the liquid and gas phases.
    6. There are attractive forces between atoms/molecules, and these become stronger as the particles move closer together. These attractive forces are called **intermolecular forces**.

### *Properties of matter in kinetic molecular theory*

#### *properties of gases*

- Gases have **no fixed shape and fixed volume**. They assume the volume and shape of their containers.
- Gases can be easily **compressed**, the compression results in a **decrease of its volume**. This happens due to the **large spaces** between the particles of gases.
- Gases have **low densities** compared to liquids and solids,A small mass of a gas occupies a large volume and hence results in a very low density.
- Gases **exert pressure in all directions**, This pressure is due to **collisions between gas molecules** and the **walls of the container**.
- Gases easily **flow and diffuse** through one another, Particles in a gas have **high energy** and move very fast in all directions.

![[Pasted image 20250219111852.png|300]]
#### *properties of liquids*

- Liquids have a **definite volume, but have no definite shape** they assume the shape of the container, Lack of a definite shape for liquid substances arises from **low intermolecular forces** of attraction between their particle. 
- Liquids have **higher densities** than gases. Their density is a result of the close arrangement of liquid particles. 
- Liquids are **slightly compressible**. The available free space between particles in liquids is very little.
- Liquids are **fluids**. A *fluid* is a substance that can *easily flow*, fluidity of liquids is much slower than gases.

![[Pasted image 20250219113216.png|300]]

#### *properties of solids*

- Solids have a **definite shape and definite volume**, due to the presence of the **strong force of attraction** between the particles in a solid.
- Solids generally have **higher densities** than gases and liquids, the closeness of particles makes solids to have **more particles (mass) per unit volume.**
- Solids are extremely **difficult to compress** because the **high interparticle forces of attraction** between the particles of solids are very strong due to a very **short distance** between them.
- Solids are not **fluids**. Particles in a solid have low energy and vibrate in fixed positions.

## **The Gaseous State**

### *The kinetic molecular Theory of gases*

- The **kinetic molecular theory** is a simple model for understanding the **behavior of gases**. 

- Assumptions of the kinetic molecular theory of gases:

     - A gas is a collection of **particles** in constant, straight-line motion.

     - Gas particles do not **attract or repel** each other, there is no **intermolecular force** between the particles, The **particles collide** with each other and with the surfaces around them, but they bounce back from these collisions like a **billiard balls**.
     
     ![[Pasted image 20250219125055.png|300]]
     -  There is a lot of space between gas particles, Volume of the **molecules** is negligible compared to the total volume of the gas.The molecules are so far apart, the total volume of the molecules is extremely small compared with the total volume of the gas.

         - Gas particles are extremely small - smaller than the spaces between them - which is why a gas is mostly empty space.

     - The **average kinetic energy** of gas particles is proportional to the **temperature** of the gas in kelvins. This means that as the temperature increases, the particles move faster and therefore have more kinetic energy. mathematically,$$KE = \frac{1}{2} mv^2$$ where   $\text{KE} \propto \text{T} \quad(\text{in kelvin})$
     
	 - According to this formula different molecules have different velocities and, therefore, have different kinetic energies.

### *Gas Laws*

#### *introduction*

- **GAS LAWS** are mathematical rules about how a gas will behave under certain conditions.
- The first reliable measurement on the properties of gases was made by *Robert Boyle* in 1662
- The **gas laws** express **mathematical relationships** between their:
     - volume
    - Temperature
    - Pressure
    - Moles of gas

##### *Pressure*

The force applied per unit area that results from the collision of gas particles with their surrounding surfaces.

$$Pressure = \frac{force}{area}\quad = \quad P = \frac{F}{A}$$

**Pressure** is one of the measurable properties of gases. Thus, the pressure of a gas can be expressed in unit of **atmosphere, Pascal, torr, millimeter of mercury**. The SI unit of pressure is *Pascal (Pa)*.

  $$1Pa = 1N/m^2$$
 $$1atm = 760mmHg = 76cmHg = 760torr = 101,000Pa = 101KPa$$

##### *Volume*

Is a *space* taken by a body, The SI unit of volume is the *cubic meter ($m^3$).* Volume is also expressed in **cubic centimeter (**$cm^3$**) and cubic decimeter ($dm^3$)**. Other common units of volume are **milliliter (mL) and liter (L).**

$$
1 \text{ cm}^3 = (1 \times 10^{-2} \text{ m})^3 = 1 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m}^3  
$$

$$
1 \text{ dm}^3 = (1 \times 10^{-1} \text{ m})^3 = 1 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^3 = 1 \text{ L}  
$$

A liter is equivalent to one cubic decimeter, as follows:  

$$
1 \text{ L} = 1000 \text{ mL} = 1000 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ dm}^3  
= 0.001m^3$$

##### *Temperature*

- Is the hotness or the coldness of a body.
- Three temperature scales are commonly used. These are**°F (degrees Fahrenheit)**, **°C (degrees Celsius)** and **K (Kelvin).**
- **K(kelvin)** is the SI-unit of temperature 
$$
\text{Celsius to Fahrenheit:} \quad F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32
$$

$$
\text{Fahrenheit to Celsius:} \quad C = \frac{5}{9} (F - 32)
$$

$$
\text{Celsius to Kelvin:} \quad K = C + 273.15
$$

$$
\text{Kelvin to Celsius:} \quad C = K - 273.15
$$

$$
\text{Fahrenheit to Kelvin:} \quad K = \frac{5}{9} (F - 32) + 273.15
$$

$$
\text{Kelvin to Fahrenheit:} \quad F = \frac{9}{5} (K - 273.15) + 32
$$

- **Molar Volume and Standard Conditions**: The conditions of a pressure of**1 atmosphere** and a temperature of **0°C (273 K)** are called **standard temperature and pressure (STP)** for gases. 
- At STP the volume of one mole of any gas is **22.4 liters**. This volume is known as the **molar volume** of gases.


#### *1. Boyle's Law*

The first quantitative experiments on gases were performed by *Robert Boyle* 
Boyle’s law describes the relationship between **pressure and volume** at constant temperature and amount of gas.

>[!important]- Boyle's Law
>states that "At constant temperature, the **volume** of a fixed amount of a gas is **inversely proportional** to its **pressure**."
>
Mathematically, $V \propto \frac{1}{P}$ (at constant T and n)
$$V = k \cdot \frac{1}{P} \implies V \cdot P = k$$
$$\therefore V_1 P_1 = V_2 P_2$$

- Boyle studied the relationship between the pressure of the trapped gas and its volume.
- He discovered that at constant temperature doubling the pressure on a sample of gas reduces its volume by one-half, and tripling the gas pressure reduces its volume to one-third of the original. Generally, the **volume of a gas decreases, as the pressure on the gas increases**
- When you **decrease volume**, gas molecules have **less free space to move**, so they **collide more often**, increasing pressure.

#### *2. Charles' Law*

Following Boyle’s findings, other scientists continued to study the properties of gases. One of these scientists was *Jacques Charles*

Charles found that the **volume of a gas** at constant pressure **increases linearly** with the **temperature** of the gas.

At temperatures below*0K*, the extrapolated volumes of gases would become **negative**. However, in reality a gas cannot have a negative volume, this implies that 0 K has a special significance. 0 K (-273 °C) is considered as *absolute zero*, which is theoretically the lowest attainable temperature, but practically cannot be attainable.

The **volume of a gas** and **Kelvin temperature** are **directly proportional** to each other. For example, doubling the Kelvin temperature causes the volume of a gas to double, and reducing the Kelvin temperature by half causes the volume of a gas to decrease by half. This relationship between Kelvin temperature and the volume of a gas is known as *Charles’ law*.


>[!important]- Charles' Law
states that the **volume of a fixed mass** of gas at **constant pressure** varies
directly with the **Kelvin temperature**
Mathematically, $V \propto T$ (at constant P and n)
$$V = kT \implies \frac{V}{T} = k$$ $$\therefore \frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$$

If the pressure is constant (e.g., in a balloon or piston system), the container must **increase in volume** to allow the particles to keep exerting the same force per unit area (maintaining pressure constant).

#### *3. Gay Lussac's Law*

Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac studied how the **pressure and temperature** of a fixed amount of gas at constant volume are related.

The relationship that he established is called **Gay-Lussac’s law** or the **pressure-temperature law**.

>[!important]- Gay Lussac's Law
>
>It states that at constant volume, **pressure** of a fixed amount of a gas **varies directly** with the **temperature**. 
>Mathematically: $P \propto T$ (at constant V and n)
$$P = kT \implies \frac{P}{T} = k$$ $$\therefore \frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$$



#### *4. Combined Gas Law*

A sample of a gas can undergo simultaneous changes in **temperature, pressure, and volume**. In such cases, the three variables should be considered at the same time.

The three gas laws we have learned can be brought together into a single equation known as the **combined gas law**. Is the Combination of **Boyle's Law**, **Charles' Law** and **Gay Lussac's law.

Mathematically, 

**Boyle's Law**: $V \propto \frac{1}{P}$ (at constant Temperature).

**Charles' Law**: $V \propto T$ (at constant Pressure).

**Gay Lussac's law**: $P \propto T$

So, $$V = \frac{kT}{P}$$

$$\therefore\frac{V_1P_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2P_2}{T_2}$$

#### *5. Avogadro's Law*

**Amedeo Avogadro** suggested that at the same temperature and pressure, equal **volumes** of gases contain equal **numbers of moles**.
This observation is called **Avogadro’s law**

>[!important]- Avogadro's Law
>
_States that the volume_ of a **gas** is _directly_ proportional to the number of molecules (**moles** of the gases) at a constant temperature and pressure.
Mathematically: $V \propto n$( at a constant T and P) $$V = Kn$$ $$k = \frac{V}{n}$$  $$\therefore \frac{V_1}{n_1}=\frac{V_2}{n_2}$$

This equation states that for a gas at constant temperature and pressure, the **volume is directly proportional to the number of moles** (molecules) of gas. In other words,*equal numbers of molecules of different gases compared at the same temperature and pressure occupy equal volumes.*

The volume occupied by a mole of gas at STP, is called the **standard molar volume** it is a constant for all gases 

the **standard molar volume** of an ideal gas is equal to*22.4 L per mole* at STP. For an ideal gas: **1 mol gas = 22.4 L gas**




#### *6. Ideal Gas Law*

An **ideal gas** is a hypothetical gas that **obeys the all gas laws**, *Real gases* only obey the **ideal gas** laws closely at **high temperature and low pressure**

_it Is the **Combination** of the three laws or the combination of **Combined gas law** and **Avogadro's Law**._

Mathematically:

**Boyle's Law**: $V \alpha \frac{1}{P}$ (at constant Temperature).

**Charles' Law**: $V \alpha T$ (at constant Pressure).

**Avogadro's Law**: $V \alpha n$ (at constant conditions).

$$\therefore V \alpha \frac{Tn}{P} \text{ (at constant R)}$$

$$V = R \frac{Tn}{P}$$
$$\boxed{VP = TRn}$$

- **R** is the proportionality constant and is called the **Universal Gas Constant**, which is equal to **0.082 at STP** thus, R=0.082$\frac{L.atm}{mol.K}$ at STP
- An **ideal gas** is a gas whose pressure, volume and temperature behavior can be completely explained by the **ideal gas equation**.

#### *7. Graham's Law of Diffusion*

_Diffusion_ is the expansion of gas at a given space. **Diffusion** is the spreading out of particles from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration until evenly distributed.

![[Pasted image 20250219214806.png|500]]

**Thomas Graham**, a Scottish chemist, studied the *rate of diffusion of different gases.* He found that gases having *low densities diffuse faster than gases that have higher densities*. Based on his observations he postulated the relationship between the **density of a gas and its rate of diffusion**, which is known as **Graham’s law of diffusion**.

_Graham's Law of Diffusion_ states that the rate of diffusion $R$ is **inversely proportional** to the root of the density of the substance or the molar mass of the substance at constant temperature and pressure. 

Mathematically:

$$r \propto \sqrt{\frac{1}{d}} \quad \text{or} \quad r \propto {\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}}}$$

So, for a given two gases (Gas 1 and 2):

$$r_1 \propto \sqrt{\frac{1}{d_1}} \quad \text{and} \quad r_2 \propto \sqrt{\frac{1}{d_2}}$$

$$\therefore \frac{r_1}{r_2} = \sqrt{\frac{d_2}{d_1}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{r_1}{r_2} = \sqrt{\frac{M_2}{M_1}}$$

The rate at which a gas diffuses is also inversely proportional to the time it takes to move a certain distance. Mathematically:
$$ r\propto \frac{1}{t}$$

$$\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{t_2}{t_1} = \sqrt{\frac{M_2}{M_1}}$$
