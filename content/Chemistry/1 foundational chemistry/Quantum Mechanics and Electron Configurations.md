## **Electromagnetic Radiation and atomic Spectra** 
### *Electromagnetic Radiation*

- In 1873, **James Clerk Maxwell** proposed that light consists of **electromagnetic waves**.
  - According to him, **EM** waves have an **electric field** and **magnetic field** components.
  - The two components vibrate into two *mutually perpendicular* planes.
  ![[Pasted image 20240914204547.png|300]]

- The emission and transmission of *energy* in the form of **EM** wave is known as **EM Radiation**.

- **EM** waves have three primary characteristics: 

  - **Wavelength**
  - **Frequency**
  - **Speed**

### Wavelength
- Denoted by $\lambda$.
- Is the distance the wave travels during one cycle.
- Expressed in meters (m).

### Frequency
- Denoted by $\nu$.
- It is the number of cycles the wave undergoes per second, expressed in units of $$\frac{1}{\text{seconds}}$$ or $\frac{1}{s}$ (also known as hertz,$Hz$).
  
  ![[Pasted image 20240914204847.png|400]]$$\nu = \frac{4 \text{cycles}}{1 \text{second}} = 4 \text{ Hz}$$

### Speed (c)
- Depends on two factors: wavelength and frequency.
- It is the product of its wavelength and frequency.
  $$c = \lambda \nu$$
- In vacuum, EM waves travel $$3 \times 10^8m/s$$ which is a physical constant known as **speed of light**.

- **EMR** comes in a broad range of frequencies called the **Electromagnetic Spectrum**.

# The Quantum Theory and Photon

In 1900s, a German physicist, **Max Planck**, came with a new view of matter and energy.

He proposed that *Energy*, like *matter*, is **discontinuous**.

According to him, atoms could only emit or absorb *Energy* only in a discrete quantity, like small packages or bundles. He called those packages **Quanta** for a quantum, and he said the *Energy* of a quantum is proportional to the frequency of the radiation.

$$E \propto \nu \rightarrow E = h \nu$$

- Where *h* is the Planck constant.
- *ν* is the frequency of the radiation.

$$ h = 6.64 \times 10^{-34} Js $$

- Since $\nu = \dfrac{c}{\lambda}$, the equation can be written as:

$$E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$$

- A system can only transfer *Energy* in the form of *Quanta*, so *Energy* has a particulate nature (particle properties).

## The Photoelectric Effect

In 1905, Albert Einstein used the **Quantum Theory** to explain the **Photoelectric Effect**.

- The PE Effect is a phenomenon in which electrons are ejected from a surface of a certain material exposed to light, albeit at a minimum frequency called the **threshold frequency**  $\nu_0$.

- This can only be explained by assuming that EMR is quantized (consists of photons), and the threshold frequency represents the minimum *Energy* required to remove the electron from the metal surface.
- Photons are particles of light, so the minimum *Energy* required to remove an electron is:

  $$E_0 = h \nu_0$$

- Where $E_0$ is the minimum *Energy* (or **work function**) and $ν_0$ is the threshold frequency.
- A photon less than the minimum *Energy* (*E_0*) and the threshold frequency (ν_0) cannot remove an electron. This means:
  
  $E < E_0$ and $\nu < \nu_0$

- On the other hand, if the *Energy* of one photon is greater than the minimum *Energy* and the frequency is greater than the threshold frequency, this means:

  $E > E_0$ and $ν > ν_0$

- Then the *Energy* is excess and it will be used as **Kinetic Energy** for the electron, $E_0$ is the work function, *E* is the energy of the $\gamma$(photon), and *hν_0* is the min Energy:

  $$KE = \dfrac{1}{2} m_e \nu^2 = h\nu - h\nu_0$$

- Intensity of light is the measure of the no _photons presence in given part of **Beam**.
  so a greater _intensity_ means a more photons are available to remove an **electron**.

- # The Bohr Model of the Hydrogen Atom
  
  In **1913, Niels Bohr** explained why the orbiting electrons _do not radiate_ energy as it moves around the nucleus. 
  
  **Bohr** used the idea about _quantized energy_ and proposed the following assumptions:
  
  1. The electrons in a hydrogen atom travel around the nucleus in a circular orbit.
  
  2. The energy of the electron in an atom is proportional to its distance from the nucleus. The further the electron from the nucleus, the more energy it has.
  
  3. Only a limited number of orbits with certain energies are allowed. This means the orbits are quantized.
  
  4. The only orbits that are allowed are those in which the spin (the angular momentum) of the particle is an integral multiple of $\frac{h}{2\pi}$.
  
  5. As long as an electron stays at its orbits, it neither gains nor loses energy.
  
  6. The electron moves to a higher energy orbit only by absorbing energy in the form of light and emitting energy as it drops to a lower state.
	- The energy of the light (photon) absorbed or emitted is exactly equal to the difference in the energies of the two orbits.
	  
	  Consider a hydrogen atom with a velocity **V** circulating the nucleus in an orbit of radius **r**, then the total energy of the electron is the sum of the kinetic energy (energy of movement) and potential energy (energy of position):
	  
	  $$
	  E = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{e^2}{r}
	  $$
	  
	  For an electron to stay orbiting at its orbit, then the centripetal force $\frac{e²}{r}$ must equal to the centrifugal force $\frac{mv^2}{r}$  :
	  
	  $$
	  \frac{e^2}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r} \quad \text{(Eq.1.1)}
	  $$
	  
	  He introduced an additional requirement that the spin (the angular momentum) $(mvr)$ must be equal to the integral multiple of $h/2\pi$. This requirement is called the **Quantum Condition**.
	  
	  $$
	  mvr = n \cdot \frac{h}{2\pi}
	  $$
	  
	  Solving for **V**:
	  
	  $$
	  V = \frac{n h}{2 \pi mr}
	  $$
	  
	  Solving for **r** in eq.1.1,
	  
	  $$
	  r = \frac{n^2 h^2}{4 \pi^2 m e^2} = n^2 \cdot \left( \frac{h^2}{4 \pi m e^2} \right)
	  $$
- Here **n** is a positive integer, \( (n = 1, 2, 3, ...) \) and is called a _quantum number_. It is known that $\left( \frac{h^2}{4\pi me^2} \right)$ is a collection of constants, so it can be reduced into:
  
  $$
  r = n^2 a_0
  $$
  
  $$
  a_0 \text{ is equal to } 0.54\ \mathring{A} \text{. Thus,}
  $$
  $$
  r = (0.54\ \mathring{A}) n^2
  $$
  
  **For instance: For _n_=1**
  
  $$r = (0.53Å)n^2$$
  
  $$ = (0.53R)(1)$$
  
  $$ = (0.53Å)$$
  
  This is the first Bohr radius. The larger the value of _n_, the further the electron from the nucleus.
  ![[Pasted image 20240914210336.png|300]]
  
  ---
  
  **Bohr showed that the energy that electron occupies in an hydrogen atom are:**
  
  $$E_n = -\frac{R_H}{n^2}$$
  
  where $R_H$ is the Rydberg Constant for an hydrogen atom, and has a value of $-2.18 x10^{-18}.$ Thus,
  
  $$E_n = -\frac{2.18 x 10^{-18}}{n^2}$$
- The negative sign is a convention that the electron is in a lower energy state than in a free electron (an electron far from the nucleus), and it is always assigned a value of zero.
- As an electron gets closer to the nucleus (as n decreases), it becomes larger and more negative.
- The max negative value is reached when$n = 1$ which corresponds to the most stable energy state.  **We call this the ground state or ground level.**
- The stability of the hydrogen atom diminishes for _n=2,3,..._ and higher. Each of these n levels is called an Excited State of Hydrogen with a higher energy than the ground state.
  
  ---
  
  **Ground State:** is the state of the hydrogen atom at _n=1_.
- It has the lowest energy
- It is the max negative value for $E_n$.
- It is the most stable.
  
  **Excited State:** is the state of the hydrogen atom at _n=2,3,..._
- It has the highest energy as it increases.
- It is less stable than the ground state.
  
  ---
- When an electron absorbs or emits a photon, its line spectra corresponds to the transfer of an electron from a lower orbit to a higher one.
- The energy emitted when going from a higher orbital _n<sub>i</sub>_ to a lower orbital _n<sub>f</sub>_ energy level.
- Thus, the change in energy, $\Delta E$, is the difference between the final and the initial state of electron.
  
  $$ \Delta E = n_f - n_o $$
  
  $$ \Delta E = \frac{R_H}{n_i^2} - \frac{R_H}{n_f^2} = R_H \left( \frac{1}{n_f^2} - \frac{1}{n_i^2} \right) $$
- When $n_f > n_o$, then the electron has thus absorbed energy, and $\Delta E$ is positive.
- But, if $n_f < n_o$, then the electron has lost energy and $\Delta E$ is negative.

## _The Wave Particle Duality of Matter and Energy_

- In 1924, Louis de Broglie, the French physicist, proposed a surprising reason for fixing energy levels. He said:
  - If energy is particle-like, perhaps matter is a wave-like.
  - By combining Einstein's energy-mass equivalence ($E = mc^2$) with Planck's energy of any photon $(E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda})$, de Broglie derived an equation of the wavelength for an object of mass (m) and speed (v), known as **de Broglie's wavelength**:
    $$\lambda = \frac{h}{mv}$$

- The dual nature of matter and energy is known as **Wave-Particle Duality**.

## **The Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle**

- In 1927, Werner Heisenberg, the German physicist proposed the **Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle**, stating that it is not possible to know with great certainty both the position and the momentum of the electron at the same time, mathematically:
$$  \Delta x \Delta p \geq \frac{h}{4\pi} \text{      or     }  \Delta x \Delta m v \geq \frac{h}{4\pi} $$

---

- Simply this equation says that if you know the position of the particle you cannot know the momentum of that particle and vice versa.

## The Quantum Numbers

**Erwin Schrödinger (1927)**. He said that an electron or any other particle exhibiting *wave-like* properties can be described by a mathematical equation, called **Wave Function**. Denoted by $\psi$. 

* Born Rule states that the square of the wave function $\psi^2$ gives the probability of finding an electron in a certain region in space.

* So the probability of the electron being at a definite location can be calculated,

* The region where the electrons are most likely found are called **orbitals**.

* So the electron can occupy any where in the orbital at any time.

* So the electron moves very rapidly in the orbital, behaving like an *Electron Cloud*.

In Quantum mechanics, each electron is described by four quantum numbers.

- the three quantum numbers gives the wave function($\psi$)
- the fourth gives the spin(magnetic properties)

#### **The Principal Quantum Number (n)**

* It describes the main energy level or shell.

    * It might be positive integers $(n=1,2,3,... \text{etc})$

    * It denotes the size and energy of the shell in which the orbital resides.


#### The Angular Momentum Quantum Number (l)

* It designates the shape of the atomic orbitals.

* Within shells, sub-shells and sub-levels are possible. With a characteristic shape.

    * Takes a value from 0 to $n-1$.

    * l is an orbital of the same n but different l. One said to belong to different sub-shells.

    * It is also called *azimuthal or subsidiary quantum number*.

    **Example:** $n=4$
    $$ l=\{0, 1, 2, 3 \}$$

    and each value has an orbital label and shape.

  $0\quad 1\quad 2 \quad 3\quad...$
  $s \quad p \quad d \quad f \quad...$

#### **The Magnetic Quantum Number ($m_l$)**

* Also called the orbital orientation quantum number.

    * It has integral values between $-l$ and $l$, including 0.

    * The value of $m_l$ is related to the orientation of the orbital in space relative to other orbitals in the atom.

    * The number of possible $m_l$ values of orbitals for a given l is $2l + 1$

**Note**: A given energy level n has $n^2$ orbitals.
#### **Spin Quantum Number** ($M_s$)

* Refers to the spin of an electron and the orientation of the magnetic field produced by this electron spin.

- It can only take the value +1/2 or -1/2.
- Each atomic orbital cannot accommodate more than two electrons, one with $M_s= +1/2$ and another with $M_s = -1/2$.

 **Example**: $n = 2$

Find: $l, M_l, M_s$

$l$ = $n-1 \rightarrow 0, 1.$

$M_t$ = $-l, l \rightarrow -1,0,1$

$M_s$ = $±1/2.$

- This suggests the probability of finding an electrons in a spherically symmetrical volume whose origin is the nucleus, and $1s$ orbital is the smallest; $2s$ is larger than 1s and so on.

    - Regardless of $n$, all $s$ orbitals are spherical.

    - The lobes of p orbitals lie along a line with the nucleus at the center. Therefore, the three $2p$ orbitals are classified as $2p_x$, $2p\_y$, and $2p\_z$.

## Electron Configurations and Orbital Diagrams

- The electron configuration of any atom is governed by the following three principles:

### 1. Aufbau (building up) Principle

- States that electrons must first _occupy_ the __lowest energy__ level before entering the higher one.

- According to the ground state electron configuration, patterns are obtained by filling subshells like this: 
![[Pasted image 20240914211047.png|250]].

### 2. Hund's Principle

- States that equal energy orbitals (degenerate orbitals) are each occupied by a single electron before the second electron of opposite spin enters the orbital.

  _Example:_ In the three orbitals of 2$P$ (2$P_X$, 2$P_Y$, 2$P_Z$), each of them first receives one electron. Before getting, second opposite spin electron.

### 3. Pauli's Exclusion Principle

- States that no two electrons can have the same four quantum numbers, meaning one of them must have one different quantum numbers.
