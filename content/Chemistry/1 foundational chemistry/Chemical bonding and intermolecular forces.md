### Chemical Bonding

- *is an electrical attraction between the positive charged nuclei and the negatively charged electrons of atoms that forms when the force of attraction is stronger than the force of repulsion*

- **Is the attractive force that binds atoms together in a molecule.**

- In 1916, G. N. Lewis concluded that atoms combine in order to achieve more stable electronic configuration resulting in molecules or compounds.

- As independent particles, atoms are at relative high potential energy.

- when atoms interact to form chemical bonds only there outer most shell or region is in contact
- when we study chemical bonding we are primarily concerned with **valance electrons**
#### Valence electrons 

- **Valence electrons:** are electrons that exist on the most outer shell of an atom
- Noble gas atoms with *eight electrons* (except He). In the outer most Shells are **stable**. Thus, the $ns^{2}np^{6}$ electron valance structure has a maximum stability.
- Atoms containing *less than eight* electrons in the outermost shell are **unstable**. To obtain stability, those atoms tend to have *eight electrons* in the outermost shell. This leads to **Octet Rule**.
- **Octet Rule** states that atoms tend to gain, lose or share electrons until there are eight electrons on there valance shell

- Type or characteristics of the resulting arrangement depends largely on the type of chemical bonding. between atoms, these are:
  - **Ionic Bond**
  - **Covalent Bond**
  - **Metallic Bond**

# **Ionic Bonding**
- When atoms lose or gain electrons, they become *ions*.
- Ion is electrically charged particles. There are one or two types of ions: those are
  - **Positive Ions** (Cations)
  - **Negative Ions** (Anions)

Properties of metals differ from those of non-metals. A metal has 1, 2, or 3 electrons on the outermost shell.

- Metals tend to lose these electrons and become **positive charged ions**. For example, if a metal \( M \) loses one electron, it becomes an ion with a charge of +1:

$$
M \rightarrow M^+ + e^-
$$


If it loses two electrons, it becomes an ion with a charge of +2:

$$
M \rightarrow M^{2+} + 2e^-
$$

 A non-metal may have 4, 5, 6, or 7 electrons on the outer shell. Non-metals tend to gain electrons to form negatively charged ions. For example, a non-metal $( X )$ gains one electron $( e^- )$ and becomes an ion with a charge of -1:

$$
X + e^- \rightarrow X^-
$$

>[!Note]
that hydrogen can form both anion $( H^- )$ (in compounds like HCl) or cation $( H^+)$ (in compounds like NaH).

**Charge** is the same for each ion in a given group or column.
### **Ionic Bond Formation**

- When **two atoms** combine, one of the atoms gives **electrons** and becomes a **cation** and the other loses electrons to form a **anion**.

- When a **cation** and **anion** are brought closer to each other, an **electrostatic  of attraction** is set up between them. This force of attraction between the two **opposite charges** is called **Ionic Bond** and it also called **electrovalent bonds**.

- This is produced when **electrons are transferred** from the outermost shell of a **metal atom** to the outermost shell of a **nonmetal atom**.

- Let's consider the formation of the bond: When Sodium (Na) and Chlorine (Cl) react, a Sodium atom **gives** 1 valence electron. 

In order to attain the electron configuration of the nearest noble gas **(Ne)**, sodium has to lose its valence electron and form a **Sodium ion ($Na^+$)**. Chlorine has 7 electrons. By gaining 1 electron, it attains the electron configuration of **argon ($Ar$)** and forms **Chloride ion ($Cl^-$)**.

Atoms that are bound together by an **ionic bond** form **ionic compounds**. For example, **$Na^+$** and **$Cl^-$** form **Sodium Chloride ($NaCl$)**.

## **Polarity in Covalent Molecule**

- A **covalent bond** is formed when electron pairs are shared between two atoms.

- A **covalent bond** in which electrons are shared equally between the two atoms is called a **non-polar covalent bond**.  $H - H$

- A **non-polar bond** is a covalent bond in which bonding electrons are shared equally between identical atoms, resulting in a **uniform distribution of electrical charge**.

- In contrast, in the covalent bonded **HCl molecule**, the H and Cl atoms are of different elements; therefore, they do not share the bonding electrons equally.
      ![[Pasted image 20240912180602.png|70]]
- A **polar covalent bond** is one that is formed when electrons spend more time in the vicinity of one atom than the other; it is caused by the **electronegativity** of two atoms forming the bond.

- Elements with high **electronegativity** have a greater tendency to attract bonding electrons A higher tendency to attract a shared electron than elements with low electronegativity.

- In this case, **HCl**, the electronegativity of **chlorine** is greater than the **hydrogen atom**. The shared pair of electrons is more strongly attracted by a chlorine atom. As a result, chlorine forms a polar molecule: a partial negative charge ($\delta^-$) resides in the hydrogen atom, causing a partial positive charge ($\delta^+$). The **Delta** is read as "partial" or "slight."

- If a molecule forms a positive or negative end, it is said to be **polar** and possesses a dipole, dipole mean two.

## Metallic bonding

- The highest-energy orbitals of most metals are occupied by a very few electron.  
- The valence electrons of metals are not held by an individual atom; rather, they are **delocalized** and mobile (free to move).  
- Metallic bonding is formed as a result from the attraction between the metal ions and the surrounding sea of electrons.  

### Properties of Metallic Bonding
- Most metals are easy to shape due to their MALABILITY AND DUCTILITY

**malability** allows substances such as metal to be **shaped** by hammering and bending. Metals can produce thin sheets.

**Ductility** allows substances to be **drawn** or pulled into long thin pieces, such as wires.


# Intermolecular Forces

*Intermolecular forces* are **weak forces of attraction** that exist between molecules.

Intermolecular forces vary in their strength, but they are weaker than the forces that connect atoms in the molecule.

- **Dipole-Dipole Forces**
- **London Dispersion Forces**
- **Hydrogen Bonding**

### A. Dipole-Dipole Forces

Dipole-dipole forces are strong intermolecular forces in polar molecules.

A dipole force is created by equal but opposite charges separated by a short distance. A dipole is represented by an arrow with a head pointing towards a negative pole and the tail **Stating** at the **positive pole**. the dipole created by a hydrogen **chloride** molecule, which is more negative chlorine atom.
![[Pasted image 20240912183024.png|500]]
- The negative end in one polar molecule attracts the positive end in an adjacent molecule in a liquid or solid. Dipole-dipole forces occur in molecules such as ethyl alcohol and water

## **London Dispersion Forces**
All **atoms** or **molecules** including those with **dipole** moments exerts force on each other.
![[Pasted image 20240912183509.png|500]]

- **London Dispersion Force**: Acts between all atoms and molecules.

- They are the only forces that exist between **noble gas** atoms and non polar molecules in fact it works in the low boiling point of both
- their strength increases with the number of electrons in the interacting atom or molecule Hence, it increases with the atomic number and molar mass.
- This can be seen by comparing the boiling points of gases (He and Ar), (H2 and O2), and (Cl2 and Br2).

## **Hydrogen Bonding**

- It is a particular type of intermolecular force arising when a hydrogen atom is bonded to highly electronegative elements, **fluorine**, **oxygen**, and **nitrogen** (F, O, N).
- Hydrogen bonding is a particular type of dipole-dipole interaction between Polar compounds, Compounds with large electronegativity differences between the hydrogen and the fluorine, oxygen, and nitrogen have the bond connecting them highly polar.
- This polarity gives the hydrogen atom a positive charge.
- Hydrogen bonding is responsible for unusual high boiling points of some compounds, such as hydrogen hydrogen fluoride (HF), water (H2O), and ammonia (NH3).

- Hydrogen bonds are usually represented by dotted lines connecting the hydrogen atom to the unshared electron pairs of the electronegative atom to which it is attracted.
