### **Mathematical and Theoretical Explanation of the Gold Foil Experiment (Rutherford’s Experiment)**

The **Gold Foil Experiment**, conducted by **Ernest Rutherford** in 1909, was a critical experiment that disproved the "plum pudding model" of the atom and led to the discovery of the atomic nucleus. The experiment was carried out by Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden under Rutherford’s supervision, and it involved the scattering of alpha particles by a thin sheet of gold foil.

#### **1. The Setup and Observations**

The experiment involved:
- A **thin gold foil** (about 1000 atoms thick).
- A source of **alpha particles** ($\alpha$ particles), which are positively charged and relatively massive (helium nuclei).
- A **fluorescent screen** surrounding the gold foil to detect the scattered alpha particles by emitting flashes of light where the particles hit.
  
The goal was to observe how alpha particles, which were positively charged and emitted from a radioactive source, interacted with the gold atoms.

**Observations:**
- Most of the alpha particles passed straight through the foil with no deflection.
- A small fraction of the particles were deflected at large angles.
- A very tiny fraction (about 1 in 8000) were deflected back almost directly toward the source.

#### **2. Rutherford's Interpretation**

Rutherford interpreted these surprising results using a new model of the atom. The previous **plum pudding model**, which proposed that positive charge was spread evenly throughout the atom with negatively charged electrons embedded within, could not explain these results.

Instead, Rutherford proposed that:
- **Most of the atom’s mass and positive charge is concentrated in a very small, dense central region**, called the **nucleus**.
- The majority of the atom’s volume is empty space, allowing most alpha particles to pass through without interaction.
- The large deflections occurred when alpha particles passed close to, or collided with, the **nucleus**, which is positively charged and repelled the positively charged alpha particles.

#### **3. Mathematical Explanation of Alpha Particle Scattering**

Rutherford applied **classical mechanics** and **Coulomb's law** to explain the scattering of alpha particles by the nucleus. The key idea was that the repulsive force between the positively charged alpha particles and the nucleus could deflect the alpha particles if they came close to the nucleus.

##### **Coulomb's Law**:
The force between two charged particles is given by Coulomb’s law:
$$
F = \frac{1}{4 \pi \epsilon_0} \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{r^2}
$$
Where:
- $Z_1$ is the atomic number of the gold nucleus (for gold, $Z = 79$),
- $Z_2$ is the charge number of the alpha particle ($Z_2 = 2$ for helium),
- $e$ is the elementary charge ($1.60 \times 10^{-19}$ C),
- $r$ is the distance between the alpha particle and the nucleus,
- $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space ($8.85 \times 10^{-12} \, \text{C}^2/\text{N} \cdot \text{m}^2$).

This equation describes the electrostatic repulsion between the alpha particle and the nucleus, causing the deflection.

##### **Scattering Angle and Impact Parameter**:

The **scattering angle** ($\theta$) of the alpha particles is determined by how close the particle comes to the nucleus, known as the **impact parameter** ($b$). The impact parameter is the perpendicular distance between the initial velocity vector of the alpha particle and the center of the nucleus. The closer an alpha particle passes to the nucleus (i.e., smaller $b$), the greater the deflection.

Rutherford derived the relationship between the scattering angle and the impact parameter using classical mechanics. He showed that the scattering angle ($\theta$) is related to the initial kinetic energy of the alpha particle and the impact parameter:
$$
\theta = 2 \tan^{-1} \left( \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4 \pi \epsilon_0 m v^2 b} \right)
$$
Where:
- $m$ is the mass of the alpha particle,
- $v$ is the velocity of the alpha particle,
- $b$ is the impact parameter.

This equation shows that the scattering angle increases as the impact parameter decreases, meaning that particles that pass closer to the nucleus experience stronger repulsive forces and are deflected more.

##### **Kinetic Energy and Closest Approach**:
The closest distance an alpha particle can approach the nucleus, known as the **distance of closest approach** ($r_{\text{min}}$), occurs when the kinetic energy of the alpha particle is entirely converted into electrostatic potential energy at the point of closest approach. This can be derived from energy conservation:
$$
\frac{1}{2} mv^2 = \frac{1}{4 \pi \epsilon_0} \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{r_{\text{min}}}
$$

Solving for $r_{\text{min}}$ gives:
$$
r_{\text{min}} = \frac{1}{4 \pi \epsilon_0} \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{\frac{1}{2} mv^2}
$$

This equation allows us to calculate the closest distance the alpha particle reaches before being repelled by the nucleus. For most alpha particles, $r_{\text{min}}$ is much larger than the radius of the nucleus, which explains why the majority of particles pass through without being deflected.

#### **4. Rutherford’s Nuclear Model of the Atom**

From the results of the gold foil experiment, Rutherford proposed a new model of the atom, known as the **nuclear model**:
- The **nucleus** contains almost all the mass of the atom and is positively charged. The size of the nucleus is incredibly small compared to the overall size of the atom (around $10^{-15}$ m in diameter).
- The **electrons** are distributed around the nucleus at relatively large distances (on the order of $10^{-10}$ m).
- Most of the atom is **empty space**, allowing the alpha particles to pass through without significant deflection.

#### **5. Theoretical Significance**

The gold foil experiment demonstrated for the first time that atoms are not solid, indivisible spheres (as suggested by the "plum pudding model"), but instead have a tiny, dense nucleus at their center. This led to the **nuclear theory of the atom** and laid the foundation for the development of quantum mechanics and modern atomic theory.

Key theoretical takeaways:
- **Deflection by the Coulomb force**: The deflection of alpha particles is due to the electrostatic repulsion between the positive charges in the nucleus and the alpha particles.
- **Conservation of Energy**: The closest approach of the alpha particles to the nucleus is governed by the conversion of kinetic energy into potential energy.
- **Rutherford Scattering Formula**: The mathematical derivation of the scattering angles based on the impact parameter provided quantitative evidence for the existence of a small, dense nucleus.

#### **6. Conclusion**

The gold foil experiment was a turning point in atomic physics, showing that the atom is mostly empty space with a dense, positively charged nucleus at the center. The experiment also introduced the concept of scattering due to Coulomb forces, leading to the derivation of the **Rutherford scattering formula** and laying the foundation for the **nuclear model of the atom**, a cornerstone of modern physics.
