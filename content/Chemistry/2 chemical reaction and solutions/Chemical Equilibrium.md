## reversable and irreversible reactions

- If two gases were to react in an open vessel or container the gaseous reactants would completely convert into the product but the products would not go back into the original reactants thus the reaction cannot be reversed or it is **irreversible**.

- On the other hand if the gases reacted in a closed vessel then the reactants would change into a product and equivalently the products would convert into a reactant thus it can be reversed it is **reversable**

Many reactions go to **completion**, those are reactions where the reactants completely convert into a product and the amount of reactants left after the reaction if there is any is very small this kind of reaction is known as **==Irreversible reactions or one-way reactions==** and they are denoted by a **single arrow($\rightarrow$)**

- for instance the combustion of carbon by oxygen is an irreversible reaction

$$ C_2(g) + O_2(g) \rightarrow CO_2(g) $$

conversely, some reactions do not go to completion, where the reactants and the products interconvert this type of reaction is know as **==reversible reaction or two-way reactions==** this reactions include both the **forward reaction and the reverse(backward) reactions**

- the reactions in which the reactants form the product is known as **the forward reaction**

- the reaction where the products convert back into the original reactants is known as **reversible reaction**

this reaction is denoted by the double arrow($\rightleftharpoons$) which indicates the reaction that goes from the reactants to the products to the right and from the products to the reactants to the left

the general form goes like this: $$ aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD $$

for example the formation ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen is a reversible reaction

$$ N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g) $$

## attainment of chemical equilibria

for a reversible reaction, the rate at which the reactants form the products id known as **==rate of the forward reaction($r_f$)==** and the rate at which the products convert back into the reactants is known as **==the rate of the reverse reaction($r_r$)==**.

At the beginning the concentration of the reactants is high meaning the rate of the forward reaction is fast or at a Hight rate as $rate \propto concentration$ but gradually the rate decreases as the concentration of the reactants decrease converting into the product, the reverse reaction has very low rate as the concentration of the products at the beginning is low but as time goes on the concentration of the products increase, increasing the rate until **equilibrium** is established, where the amount of reactants converting into a product equals to the amount of product converting into a reactant meaning when the rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the reverse reactions.

Thus, equilibrium is attained when the rate of the forward reaction equals to the rate of the reverse reaction.

> **==Chemical Equilibrium==** is the state by which the rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the reverse reaction

### Conditions for the attainment of chemical equilibrium

> A chemical equilibria is a **dynamic equilibria**

- a **dynamic equilibria** means that reactants and products are interconverting continuously even though the the overall composition of the reaction doesn't change.
- once the equilibria is established any change in one direction is balanced by an opposing direction

In a closed vessel a reversible reaction reaches dynamic equilibria thus chemical equilibria by:

- the concentration of the reactants corresponds to the rate of the forward reaction and the concentration of the products corresponds to the rate of the reverse reaction.

- initially, the rate of the forward reaction is fast as the the concentration of the reactants is high but as the concentration of the products is law the rate of the reverse reaction is very slow

- as the reaction progresses the reactants convert into the product decreasing the concentration of the reactants and conversely increasing the products therefore the rate of the forward reaction slows as the the rate of reverse gets faster, at some point the amount by which the reactants convert into a product will equal to the amount by which the product convert back into the reactants this is when **chemical equilibrium** is established.

Note that when equilibrium is reached it is not concentration of the reactants and the products that is equal but instead it is the rate of the forward and the reverse that is equal.

generally here are some of the characteristics of chemical equilibria:

- the reaction is always **reversible** in nature
- the concentration of the reactants and the products remain constant once equilibrium is established
- the rate of the forward reaction is equal to the reverse
- the reaction always takes place in a closed vessel or a closed system
- equilibrium is dynamic in nature
- the concentration of the products and the reactants are not necessarily equal at equilibrium

## equilibrium expressions and equilibrium constant

### The law of mass action

mathematically chemical equilibrium constant is expressed based on **the law of mass action**

> it states that the rate at which A and B combines is equal to the product of the concentration of A and B raised to the power of their respective coefficients in the balanced chemical reaction

the general form for a reversible reaction is:

$$ aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD $$

The rate of the forward reaction is dependent on the concentration of A and B and the rate of the reverse reactions is dependent on the concentration of C and D.

so using the **rate law**: $rate = K[A]^a$ where k is the **rate constant** and a is the **order** of the reaction.

We know that:

$$ r_f \propto [A]^a[B]^b $$

$$ r_r \propto [C]^c[D]^d $$

where [-] indicates the concentration of A,B,C and D.

So from the rate law we know that the proportionality constant is the **rate constant**

$$ r_f =K_f [A]^a[B]^b $$

$$ r_r =K_r [C]^c[D]^d $$

where $K_f$ is the rate constant for the forward reaction and $K_r$ is the rate constant for the reverse reaction.

equilibrium is attained when the rate of the forward and the reverse are equal:

$$ r_f = r_r $$

$$ K_f [A]^a[B]^b = K_r [C]^c[D]^d $$

$$ \frac{K_f}{K_r} = \frac{[C]^c[D]^d}{[A]^a[B]^b} = K_{eq} $$

Thus the ratio of the rate constants for the forward and reverse is equal to the **==equilibrium constant($K_{eq}$)==**

Depending on what we are taking as a measurement for the rate the equilibrium expression can be written in two forms:

- when $K_{eq}$ is measured in terms of concentration it is shown as $K_{c}$

- when $K_{eq}$ is measured in terms of partial pressure on gaseous reactants and products it is shown as $K_{p}$

#### **Equilibrium constant expression in terms of $K_{c}$**

there are two types of reactions depending on the sate of matter of the reactants and products:

- when the reactants and products have the same phase or state it is known as a **homogeneous reaction**
- wen there is more than one physical state between the reactants and products it is known as a **heterogeneous reaction**

When writing the equilibrium constant expression for $K_{c}$ or $K_{p}$ the following rules must be followed:

- the concentration or partial pressure of the product is written at the numerator
- the concentration or partial pressure of the reactants is written at the denominator
- each species of the reactant or the product are raised to a power equal to their coefficient in the balanced reaction
- for $K_{c}$ species with an aqueous(aq) or gaseous(g) states are involved in the expression and for $K_{p}$ only gaseous(g) species are involved pure liquid and solid are not involved in either of the expressions.

for a general reaction at equilibrium: $$ aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD $$

the expression for $K_{c}$ is: $$ K_{c} = \frac{[C]^c[D]^d}{[A]^a[B]^b}$$

and the unit of $K_{c}$ depends upon the number of mole of reactants and products there are:

$$

K_c = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b} = \frac{(\text{mol} \cdot \text{L}^{-1})^c \cdot (\text{mol} \cdot \text{L}^{-1})^d}{(\text{mol} \cdot \text{L}^{-1})^a \cdot (\text{mol} \cdot \text{L}^{-1})^b} = (\text{mol} \cdot \text{L}^{-1})^{(c + d) - (a + b)}

$$

So the unit of $K_{c}$ is in the units of Molarity as $(\text{mol} \cdot \text{L}^{-1})^{\Delta{n}}$ where $\Delta{n}=(c+d)-(a+b)$

#### **Equilibrium constant expression in terms of $K_{p}$**

This is the case where all the reactants and products are gaseous and is written in terms of partial pressure and denoted by $K_{p}$ for the previous general dynamic reaction $K_{p}$ is written as:

$$ K_{p}= \frac{(P_c)^c(P_D)^d}{(P_A)^a(P_B)^b} $$

the unit of $K_{p}$ is in the units of pressure as: $(atm)^\Delta{n}$ where $\Delta{n}=(c+d)-(a+b)$

Note that when we write equilibrium expressions for heterogeneous reactions we don't include the concentration of pure liquid and solid in the expression as the concentration of solids and liquids doesn't change with a change in temperature.

- we use aqueous and gaseous states for $K_{c}$
- and only gaseous states for $K_{p}$

#### **The Relationship between $K_{p}$ and $K_{c}$**

For the general reaction

$$

aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD

$$

$K_c$ and $K_p$ expressions are:

$$

K_c = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b} = \frac{C_C^c C_D^d}{C_A^a C_B^b}

$$

$$

K_p = \frac{[P_C]^c [P_D]^d}{[P_A]^a [P_B]^b}

$$

Assume that all gaseous species behave like an ideal gas. Thus, according to the ideal gas equation, the partial pressure (P) of a gas is given by:

$$

P = \frac{nRT}{V} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{n}{V} = C

$$

Where $n$ is amount of the gaseous species in moles, $R$ is gas constant, $T$ is temperature in kelvin, $V$ is volume of the reaction mixture and $C$ is concentration in molarity.

Thus, partial pressure of each species for the above general reaction is:

$P_A = \frac{n_A RT}{V} = C_A RT$

$P_B = \frac{n_B RT}{V} = C_B RT$

$P_C = \frac{n_C RT}{V} = C_C RT$

$P_D = \frac{n_D RT}{V} = C_D RT$

$$

K_p = \frac{[n_C RT / V]^c [n_D RT / V]^d}{[n_A RT / V]^a [n_B RT / V]^b} = \frac{C_C^c C_D^d}{C_A^a C_B^b} (RT)^n

$$

Where $\Delta n = (c + d) - (a + b)$

for gaseous species.

$$

K_p = K_c (RT)^{\Delta n}

$$

When the number of gaseous reactants and products are equal then, $a + b = c + d$

Therefore, $\Delta n = 0$ and, hence $K_p = K_c$

If $\Delta n > 0$ then there will be more gaseous products than reactants thus $K_{p} > K_{c}$.
If $\Delta n < 0$ then there will be fewer gaseous products than reactants thus $K_{p} <K_{c}$.

Consider the reactions in which the product species in one equilibrium system are involved in the second equilibrium process.

**(i)**

$\text{A} + \text{B} \rightleftharpoons \text{C} + \text{D}$

**(ii)**

$\text{C} + \text{D} \rightleftharpoons \text{E} + \text{F}$

The equilibrium constant expressions for reaction (i) and (ii), respectively, are:

$$

K_1 = \frac{[C][D]}{[A][B]} \quad \text{and} \quad K_2 = \frac{[E][F]}{[C][D]}

$$

The overall reaction is given by the sum of the two reactions:

$$

\begin{aligned}

\text{A} + \text{B} &\rightleftharpoons \text{C} + \text{D} \\

\text{C} + \text{D} &\rightleftharpoons \text{E} + \text{F} \\

\hline

\text{A} + \text{B} &\rightleftharpoons \text{E} + \text{F}

\end{aligned}

$$

**Overall reaction:**

$$

\text{A} + \text{B} \rightleftharpoons \text{E} + \text{F}

$$

The equilibrium constant expression for the overall reaction is:

$$

K_{\text{overall}} = \frac{[E][F]}{[A][B]} = K_1 \times K_2 = \frac{[C][D]}{[A][B]} \times \frac{[E][F]}{[C][D]}

$$

Note that if a reaction is the sum of two or more reactions, the equilibrium constant of the overall reaction is equal to the product of the equilibrium constants of the individual reactions.

That is:

$$

K_{\text{overall}} = K_1 \times K_2 \times K_3 \dots

$$

| **Operation**                | **Effect on Equilibrium Constant $K$**                  |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Flip the reaction           | Take reciprocal: $K' = \frac{1}{K}$                     |
| Multiply reaction by $n$    | Raise to power: $K' = K^n$                              |
| Multiply reaction by $\frac{1}{n}$ | Take root: $K' = K^{1/n}$                           |
| Add reactions               | Multiply: $K_{\text{overall}} = K_1 \cdot K_2 \cdot \dots$ |
