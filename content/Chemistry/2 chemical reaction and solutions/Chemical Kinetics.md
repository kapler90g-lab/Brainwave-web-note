#unit4 #grade11 #Chemical_kinetics  

>[!Note]- **chemical kinetics**
>
>the **area of chemistry** that is concerned with the study of **reaction rates** is called *chemical kinetics*. 
>
>**chemical kinetics**—the study of reaction rates and how we can speed them up, slow them down, or even stop them entirely.
>- At its core, chemical kinetics is about answering **two fundamental questions**: 
 >    1. **How fast does a reaction happen?** (Reaction rate)
 >    2. **What controls that speed?** (Reaction mechanisms & influencing factors)
 >   
 >   The word “**kinetic**” suggests **movement or change**. Chemical kinetics refers to the rate of reaction, which is the **changeover times in the concentration** of a reactant or a product.

## **Rate of a Reaction**

*The rate of a chemical reaction* measures the **change in concentration** of a reactant or a product per unit time.  this means that the rate of a reaction determines how fast the concentration of a reactant or product changes with time


In a chemical reaction, **reactants are consumed** while **products are formed** over time. This means we can track the progress of a reaction by measuring:  
- The **decrease** in the concentration of reactants, or  
- The **increase** in the concentration of products.  
![[Pasted image 20250318195856.png]]
For example, consider a simple reaction:  

$$A \rightarrow B$$  
In this reaction, molecule **A** is converted into molecule **B** over time. As the reaction proceeds:  
- The number (or concentration) of **A molecules decreases**.  
- The number (or concentration) of **B molecules increases**.  


The rate of a reaction is defined as the **change in concentration** of a substance over time:  

$$\text{Rate of reaction} = \frac{\Delta \text{concentration}}{\Delta \text{time}}=\frac{\Delta C}{\Delta t}$$ 
Here, **Δ (delta) represents the difference** between the initial and final concentrations over a given time period.  

This formula helps us quantify how fast a reaction is happening by looking at how quickly reactants disappear or products form.


For the reaction:  
$$A \rightarrow B$$  
We can define the reaction rate in two ways:  
1. **Based on Reactants:** Since reactants are being used up, their concentration decreases over time. The rate can be written as:  
   $$\text{Rate} = - \frac{\Delta [A]}{\Delta t}$$  
   The negative sign indicates that the concentration of A is **decreasing** over time.  

2. **Based on Products:** Since products are being formed, their concentration increases over time. The rate can be written as: 

$$\text{Rate} = \frac{\Delta [B]}{\Delta t}$$  
Since products are increasing, no negative sign is needed.  

The standard unit for reaction rate is:  

$$\text{mol} \cdot \text{L}^{-1} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$$

which means **moles per liter per second** (mol/L·s).  


 Plotting the **concentration** of reactant used or product formed against **time** on a graph, the **slope (or gradient)** of the curve represents the **reaction rate**.
$$\text{slope}=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$$
Where:
• y = concentration of reactant or product
• x = time

For more complex reactions, we must carefully consider the **stoichiometry** when writing the rate expression.  

Consider the reaction:  

$$2A \rightarrow B$$  

Here:  
- **2 moles of A** are consumed for every **1 mole of B** formed.  
- This means that A disappears **twice as fast** as B appears.  

Thus, the rate of reaction can be expressed as:  

$$\text{Rate} = -\frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta [A]}{\Delta t} = \frac{\Delta [B]}{\Delta t}$$

The **negative sign** for A indicates that its concentration is decreasing. The fraction **(1/2)** accounts for the **stoichiometric relationship** between A and B.   

For a more general reaction:  

$$aA + bB \rightarrow cC + dD$$
where:  
- **a, b, c, and d** are the stoichiometric coefficients,  
- **A and B** are reactants,  
- **C and D** are products.  

The rate expression is:  

$$\text{Rate} = -\frac{1}{a} \frac{\Delta [A]}{\Delta t} = -\frac{1}{b} \frac{\Delta [B]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{c} \frac{\Delta [C]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{d} \frac{\Delta [D]}{\Delta t}$$  
- **Reactants (A, B) have a negative sign** because they are consumed.  
- **Products (C, D) have a positive sign** because they are formed.  
- **The stoichiometric coefficients (a, b, c, d) are used** to ensure the rate expression correctly represents the reaction’s progress.
