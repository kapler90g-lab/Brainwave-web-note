### **Mixtures**  
   - Are the combinations of two or more substances in which the individual substances keep their own properties.
   - They can be separated physically and retain their properties.  
   - There are two types of mixtures:
     - **Homogeneous** 
     - **Heterogeneous**

#### Homogeneous Mixture
- Is the mixture in which the composition is **uniform throughout** the mixture.  
  - It means it does not have a visible boundary, and components are distributed throughout the mixture.
  
**Example:**  
- Salt with water.  
- Mud with water, etc.

#### Heterogeneous Mixture
- Is a mixture that consists physically distinct parts.  
- This type of mixture has at least one visible phases.

**Example:**  
- Oil and water.  
- Sand and water, etc.


# **Suspensions, Solutions and Colloids**

Mixtures can be further classified as:
- **Solutions**
- **Suspensions**
- **Colloids**

## **Suspension**
- A **suspension** is a **heterogeneous mixture**.
  - In which the solid particles are spread throughout the liquid without dissolving in it.
  
  **Example:** A mixture of **sand** and **water**.

## **Solution**
- A **solution** is a **homogeneous mixture** of solute dissolved in a solvent.
  
  Generally, the major component of the solution is called a **solvent**.
  While the minor component is called a **solute**.  

All solutions are "homogeneous" mixtures, but not all **homogeneous mixtures** are solutions.

### Properties of a Solution
- It has no visible uniform phase.
- Components (ions, molecules, atoms) do not settle upon standing or separate by filtration.
- The particles are very small that they can pass through a small pore; even a filter paper. This means that they **Can't Be** _separated_ by a **filter paper.**

### **Types of Solutions**

- There are three types of solutions depending on their physical states: **Solid** - **Liquid** - **Gases.**

#### 1. Gaseous Solutions

- Are usually described as a **gas-gas** solution where both the **solute** and the **solvent** are gases.

- The atmosphere contains many gases like $N_2, O_2, Ar, CO_2$ and some other minor components.

- The major component of the atmosphere $(Nitrogen((N)))$ is considered as a **solvent** while the other components are regarded as a **solute.**

#### 2. Liquid Solutions

- Is the solution of a **solid**, a **liquid**, and a **gas** in a **liquid.**

- **Solids**, **liquids**, and **gases** as a solute

- A **liquid** always as a **solvent.**

#### 3. Solid Solutions

- **Solid solutions** have no restrictions on what has to be a **solute.** But the **solvent** is always a **solid.**
- Some alloys, ceramics, and polymers blend form **solid solutions**.

## **colloids**

A colloid is a heterogeneous mixture in which *insoluble particles are suspended in another substance*.

#### Properties

- Colloids (*Colloidal Solutions*) appear to be homogeneous but actually, it is heterogeneous *upon a closer inspection*.
- The size of the particles in a colloidal solution is bigger than those in an actual solution, but smaller than that of a *suspension*: *1mm and 1000nm in diameter*.
- They can't be separated by *filtration*, because particles can pass through the filter paper.

- There are *two phases* in a colloid, they are known as:
    - **Dispersed Phase** and **Dispersion Medium**.
  
The component present in a smaller proportion is called a **dispersed phase** and the one present in a major proportion is called a **dispersion medium**.

- a colloid scatters a beam of light passing through it due to its fairly large size making the light path to be *visible*. This phenomenon is known as **Tyndall effect**.

#### association of Colloid 
Soap is a **special** *special* kind of molecule that can clean things.

- It has two parts: **a water-loving part (*hydrophilic*)** and **a water-hating part (*hydrophobic*).**
- When soap is mixed with water, it forms a tiny ball called **micelles**.

  - The *hydrophobic* are found **inside** the micelles.
  - The *hydrophilic* parts are found **outside** of the micelles.
  
When we wash something, the dirt will stick to the outside of the micelles (with the *hydrophobics*) and the whole thing will get washed away with water.

## The Solution Process

- A solution is formed when the solute particles are completely dissolved in the solvent.
- The formation of liquid and solid solutions requires overcoming the solute-solute and solvent-solvent interactions before the mixing step.
  the solvent and solutes are held by the intermolecular forces.

- The solution process thus involves three basic steps:
  1. **Breaking up** the solute-solute interaction.
  2. **Breaking up** the solvent-solvent interaction.
  3. **Formation** of solute-solvent interaction.

- Thus, the extent of dissolution of a solute in a solvent is governed by intermolecular forces.

### **Liquids Solutions and Intermolecular Forces of Attraction**

- The solute particles occupy positions that are normally taken by a solvent molecule.

- The ease at which the solvent molecules get replaced by a solute particle depends on the **relative strength** of the intermolecular forces, in three types of interactions:
  - **Solute-Solute Interaction.**
  - **Solvent-Solvent Interaction.**
  - **Solute-Solvent Interaction.**

- Solvents dissolve in a solute when the **intermolecular forces** of interaction between $\text{Solute molecules and solvent molecules}$ are replaced by $\text{Solvent-Solvent Interaction.}$

- The useful rule of **"like dissolves like"** states that substances with similar intermolecular forces dissolve in each other meaning that when the intermolecular forces within the solutes is similar to those within the solvent, the forces replace each other and a solution forms.

Thus, **polar molecules** dissolve in a **polar** molecules and **nonpolar molecules** dissolve in **non-polar** ________molecules.


- One of the useful classifications of molecules is **polarity**.
- A **ionic** compound has the highest polarity, forming from cation and anion.
- A **covalent compound** is polar when the bonded atoms have different electronegativities; thus, they share bonds unequally. 

In larger molecules, polarity is determined by the dipole moment (the imbalance in charge in the molecule), which in turn depends on both the shape and polarity.

**Dipole moment** is the measure of molecular polarity, given in the units: **Debye (D)**, derived from the SI unit of charge (C) and length (m).

$$
\text{D} = 3.34 \times 10^{-30} \text{C.m}
$$

- Substances with no dipole moment are non-polar.  
**Example: $\text{C}_2\text{O}_2$**

- Substances with a dipole moment are polar.  
**Example: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$**

## Solutions of Liquids in Liquids

- Solutions can also be made by mixing two **compatible** liquids.
- The liquids in lower concentration is termed as **solute**, and the higher concentration as a **solvent**.

For example, grain **alcohol** $(C_2H_5OH)$ is a polar solvent can mix with water $(H_2O)$.

- When two similar liquids (in terms of **polarity**) are paired together and are able to mix into a solution, they are said to be **miscible**.
- If not, they are termed **immiscible** (not mixable).
They both share a common characteristic and can even mix. Example: oil and water are immiscible, because one is polar and the other is nonpolar.

# The Rate of **Dissolution**

**Dissolution** is the process in which a solute dissolves in a solvent to produce a **solution**.  
And the **rate of dissolution** is the speed at which a solute dissolves in a solvent.  

This **greatly** depends on two factors:
1. The **inter-particle forces**  
2. Conditions such as **temperature** and **surface area**, also **pressure**.  

When the **solute-solvent** interaction becomes stronger than those **solute-solute** and **solvent-solvent** interactions, the rate of dissolution becomes faster.

## **Energy Changes in Solution Process**

Whenever the energy required to form a solute-solvent interaction is greater than the one required to break the solute-solute and solvent-solvent interactions, energy is released and it is caused **exothermic**, if not , **endothermic**.  
And this energy involves in the mixing step is called **solvation energy** 

### **Solvation Energy**

**Solvation** refers to the attraction and rate of interaction between the molecules of the solvent and the solute.  
**Solvation energy** is the energy released when a solute gets solvated by a solvent and **Solvation** in water is called **Hydration**.

- **Hydration Energy** is ***released*** when **solute molecules** or ions get ***solvated*** by **water molecules**.
  
- The **Heat of the Solution** is defined as the amount of heat ***absorbed*** or ***released*** during the dissolution process.

$$
Q = mC\Delta T
$$

- Where **Q** is the **enthalpy** of the solution.  
- **m** is **mass**.  
- $\Delta T (T_f - T_i)$ Change in temperature.  
- **C** is the **specific heat** characteristic of the solution.  
- The unit is ***Joules*** or ***kJ***.  
  
- The **enthalpy** of the solution can be either:  
  - **Positive** (Endothermic).  
  - **Negative** (Exothermic).

- The **molar heat of solution** ($\Delta H_{sol}$) is the heat absorbed or released when one **mole** of the substance is dissolved in water at constant pressure.

$$
\Delta H_{sol} = \frac{P}{number \text{ mol of solute}}
$$

To understand the **enthalpy change** involved during dissolution Let's consider ***three-step process*** between the two **substances**; solute and solvent.   This shows the **interaction** of solute-solvent and solvent-solvent interactions.


**Step 1.** Solute particles **separate** from each other; this needs heat to overcome the interparticle attractions.  
So it is **endothermic**.  
$$
\text{Solute (together)} + \text{Heat} \rightarrow \text{Solute (separated)} 
$$

**Step 2.** Solvent particles **separate** from each other; this also needs energy to overcome the attraction force.  
So it is **endothermic**.  
$$
\text{Solvent (together)} + \text{Heat} \rightarrow \text{Solvent (separated)} 
$$

**Step 3.** Solute and solvent particles **mix** and form a **solution**.  
These particles **attract** each other and come together.  
So, it is an **exothermic** process.  
$$
\text{Solute (separated)} + \text{Solvent (separated)} \rightarrow \text{Solution} + \text{Heat} 
$$

- The total **enthalpy change** that occurs when solute and solvent form a solution is:  
$$
\Delta H_{\text{solution}} = \Delta H_{\text{solvent}} + \Delta H_{\text{solute}} 
$$

**Endothermic** and **exothermic** **dissolution process** Depending on the relative strength of $\Delta H_{\text{solution}}$, it can be **negative** (exothermic) or **positive** (endothermic).
